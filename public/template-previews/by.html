What we make

Five kinds of work

Everything is made to commission. We keep a few finished pieces at the workshop to show the woods and carving.

I.

Dining & altar tables

Single slabs and planked tops from old-house beams, finished in oil and beeswax.

II.

Carved doors

Main doors and gates, carved with Cordillera motifs or a design you bring to us.

III.

Beds & bedroom sets

Four-posters, carved headboards and chests built with pegged joints, no screws.

IV.

Wall panels & ceilings

Pine and hardwood panelling for homes, chapels, restaurants and lodges.

V.

Restoration

Old family pieces repaired, re-glued and refinished, keeping the original patina where we can.

Our story

Carvers on Asin Road since 1962

Apo Dalisay carved bulul figures for tourists. His son carved doors. His grandchildren build whole rooms.

We are a small team of seven, and we take on about forty commissions a year so each one gets the time it needs.

Most of our wood is salvaged from old Baguio houses, lodges and churches — narra, yakal and molave that has already dried for decades.

Jun Dalisay

Third-generation master carver

Questions

Before you commission

Can I bring my own wood?+

Yes — beams from a family house are some of our favourite commissions. We'll inspect it first to see what it can become.

How long is the waiting list?+

Usually four to eight weeks before we can start, then two to three months to build.

Will reclaimed wood hold up in the lowlands?+

Yes. Old hardwood is far more stable than new lumber, and we acclimatise every piece before finishing.

Can I visit while my piece is being made?+

Please do. Many clients plan a Baguio weekend around it.
