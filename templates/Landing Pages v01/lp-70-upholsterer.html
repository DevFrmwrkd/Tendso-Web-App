PIECE 01 / THE WORKSHOPDOWN TO THE FRAME

Down to the frame, then back to life.

A good piece of furniture is a frame, and everything else — springs, webbing, stuffing, cover — is renewable. Warehouse furniture is built the other way round: a disposable frame dressed to look solid until the foam collapses and the whole thing goes to landfill. We work on the good frames, the ones worth the labour, and there are more of them in lofts and skips than anyone realises.

The workshop is in the Jewellery Quarter, at the trestles, where we strip a piece back, assess the frame, re-web and respring by hand, build up the stuffing, and recover it in your choice of fabric. Traditional horsehair-and-twine on an antique; modern foam and dacron on a mid-century piece — whatever the chair was born to be.

Bring us a grandmother's wing chair, a skip-find chesterfield, a dining suite that's outlasted two houses. We'll tell you honestly whether the frame's worth it — and when it is, we'll give you another forty years out of it for a fraction of buying new and worse.

PIECE 07 / ASKED AT THE WORKSHOPUSUALLY WITH A PHOTO

Asked at the workshop.

Anything else — ring 0121 448 9930 or send a photo over. A picture answers "is it worth it?" faster than any phone call.

Is it cheaper to reupholster or just buy new?

For a cheap flat-pack piece, buying new is usually cheaper — and we'll tell you so. For a well-made frame, reupholstery costs less than an equivalent new piece of the same quality and lasts far longer. You're paying for labour and good materials on a frame worth keeping, not for a whole new disposable thing.

How do I know if my chair is worth saving?

Send us a photo and we'll give you an honest opinion for free. The tell is the frame: solid hardwood, proper joints, some weight to it — that's worth rebuilding. Stapled softwood and chipboard usually isn't, and we won't take your money to prove it. Most inherited and older pieces are keepers.

Can I use my own fabric?

Yes, though we'll check it's suitable for upholstery first — dress fabrics and some prints don't wear well on a seat and it's kinder to say so before it's on. We also stock hundreds of fabrics and leathers in the workshop, so bring your own or choose with us; either way we'll match quantity and durability to the piece.

Do you do antiques the traditional way?

Yes — horsehair, twine, hessian, hand-tied springs and tacks, done period-correct and reversibly for pieces where the method is part of the value. We'll advise when traditional matters and when a sympathetic modern rebuild is the sensible, cheaper choice. We won't over-restore something that doesn't need it.

How long will it take?

A single dining chair, a week or two; an armchair, a few weeks; a full suite or traditional antique, longer. It depends on the work and the fabric lead time. We'll give you a realistic timeframe at quote and keep you posted — bigger jobs get progress photos so you're never wondering.

Do you collect and deliver?

Locally around Birmingham, yes — we'll collect the piece and return it when it's done. Further afield we can arrange courier for smaller items, or you're welcome to drop off and collect from the workshop. Just ask when you get in touch and we'll sort the logistics.
