ONE / THE STUDIOABOVE THE BOOKSHOP

Twelve mats, on purpose.

Still Point is deliberately small. Twelve mats means the teacher sees every body in the room — how your left shoulder guards, where your breath actually goes — and adjusts the teaching instead of performing a routine at the front.

There are no mirrors, because the point is how a pose feels, not how it photographs. There's no heat, because sweat is not evidence of progress.

The teachers have between ten and thirty years of practice each, and every one of them will tell you to do less, sooner, than any app will.

THREE / THE POINTWHAT MAKES IT STILL

Why it works.

i.

The teacher sees you.

Twelve mats, not forty. Adjustments are offered, never imposed, and "skip that pose" is respected without a lecture.

ii.

No performance.

No mirrors, no heat, no playlists building to a drop. Nothing to keep up with and no one to keep up with.

iii.

Built for ordinary bodies.

Stiff, injured, pregnant, tired, seventy — the teaching starts where you are, not where an influencer is.

SEVEN / HONEST ANSWERSASKED AT THE DOOR

Before your first class.

Anything else — ring 01603 219 884. If we're teaching, leave a message; we call back between classes.

I'm not flexible. Is that a problem?

It's the opposite of a problem — it's the reason to come. Flexibility is a side effect of practice, not an entry requirement. The stiffest person in the room is having the most useful class.

Which class should I start with?

The beginners' course if the timing works — six weeks, same group, foundations done properly. If not, any hatha class; tell the teacher you're new and they'll take it from there.

What do I need to bring?

Nothing. Mats, blocks, straps and blankets live at the studio. Wear anything you can move in; socks come off at the door.

I have an injury. Can I still practise?

Usually yes, sensibly. Tell us when you book and tell the teacher before class. For anything complicated, one session of a one-to-one first saves months of guesswork.

Is there anywhere to change?

A small changing room, one shower, and hooks that are always nearly full. Most people arrive dressed to practise.

Do you do memberships?

Class passes, no contracts, no joining fees, and passes pause when life happens. We're not a gym and don't bill like one.

What if the class is full?

Join the waitlist — it auto-promotes and a quarter of spots turn over in the last day. The Friday yin is the exception; book that one the moment the schedule opens.
