PRESSING 01 / THE MAKERA HARVEST, FERMENTED

Cider isn't apple-flavoured pop. It's a harvest, fermented.

We started Pomona & Press in 2011 among the old cider orchards of Herefordshire, tired of watching "cider" become a sugary drink made from concentrate shipped in from the other side of the world. We wanted to make it the way this county always has — from real apples, grown for cider, pressed in autumn and given the time to become something.

We work with named orchards and named varieties: bittersweets like Dabinett and Yarlington Mill for tannin and body, sharps like Browns for acidity, and we'll tell you the blend on every bottle. The fruit is milled and pressed here in autumn, fermented with its own wild yeasts, and left to do its slow work over winter. No concentrate, no added sugar, no flavourings.

The best batches are bottle-conditioned — a second fermentation in the bottle that gives a fine, natural sparkle, the way good cider and champagne share a trick. Come to the orchard tap and try them beside each other: a single-variety next to a blend, still next to sparkling. Cider has as much range as wine; most people have just never been shown.

What makes real cider different from the stuff in cans?

Most mass-market cider is made from apple juice concentrate — often imported, reconstituted with water, sweetened, carbonated and sometimes flavoured — and can legally contain a surprisingly small proportion of actual apple. Real cider like ours is pressed from whole fresh apples grown for cider, fermented (usually dry) and left to develop over months. The difference is the difference between orange squash and a fresh orange: one is engineered to a flavour, the other tastes of the fruit and the year it grew. Come and taste them side by side; you'll never unlearn it.

Why is your cider drier than what I'm used to?

Because when you ferment apple juice fully, the yeast turns nearly all the sugar into alcohol, and what's left is a dry, complex drink built on the fruit's tannin and acidity rather than added sweetness. Traditional cider is dry by nature; the sweet stuff is sweetened after the fact. That said, dry doesn't mean austere — a good bittersweet blend is round and full — and we make ciders across the spectrum, so if you like a touch of sweetness we'll point you to a medium. The trick is to taste a few and find where on the scale you sit.

What does "bottle-conditioned" mean?

It means the sparkle happens naturally in the bottle rather than being pumped in. We add a little of the cider's own sugar and yeast at bottling, seal it, and a gentle second fermentation produces fine bubbles and a touch more depth — exactly the method behind traditional champagne. It gives a softer, more integrated fizz than force-carbonation and a bit of lees character. There may be a faint natural sediment; stand the bottle upright for a day and pour gently and you'll leave it behind.

Can I visit the orchard and the press?

Yes — the orchard tap in the pressing barn is open through the season for flights, glasses and bottles, and you're welcome to walk the orchard the cider comes from. In autumn, when the press is running, you can often see (and smell) milling and pressing in full swing, which is the best time to visit. We also run booked orchard-and-press tours with a guided tasting for groups and celebrations. Check our Google profile for current opening times before you set off, as they shift with the season and the harvest.

How should I store and serve it?

Keep bottles somewhere cool and dark, and stand bottle-conditioned ones upright for a day before opening so any natural sediment settles. Serve still cider lightly chilled — too cold and you mute the flavour — and sparkling properly cold like a good fizz. A wine glass rather than a pint does the aromatic ciders far more justice than you'd expect. Some of our stronger, tannic blends will age and improve for a year or two if you can resist them; we'll tell you which are built to keep and which are best drunk young and fresh.

Do you supply pubs, shops and weddings?

Gladly — real cider deserves a wider audience and it's a good part of what we do. We supply pubs, delis, farm shops and restaurants by the case, bottle or bag-in-box, and we'll help you pick a range that suits your customers and tell its story to your staff. For weddings and events we do cider by the case or on tap, still or sparkling, with plenty of notice. Give us a call and a realistic date and we'll make sure there's enough of the good stuff to go round.
