STEEP 01 / THE MERCHANTTEA IS A HARVEST

Tea is an agricultural product. Most shops sell it like sawdust.

We opened Leaf & Ledger in 2014 because the tea aisle had given up. Row upon row of dust and fannings in bags, blended to taste the same every year, with nothing on the box about where a single leaf grew. We wanted the opposite: whole-leaf tea from gardens we can name, tasted lot by lot, sold by people who can tell you why this Darjeeling tastes of muscatel and that one doesn't.

We buy from single estates and small gardens across India, China, Japan, Taiwan and Sri Lanka, and from a handful of growers we've visited ourselves. Everything is tasted before we buy it and again before it goes on the shelf. Our blends — breakfast, afternoon, chai — are built by hand from named components, not bought pre-mixed and rebranded.

And we'll teach you to brew it. Most "bad tea" is good tea brewed wrong: water too hot, leaf too long, or a bag strangled in a mug. Come to a tasting, or just ask at the counter, and we'll send you home able to get the best out of what you buy. That's the whole job.

Why is loose-leaf tea better than teabags?

It usually isn't a moral matter, it's a physical one: most teabags are filled with "dust and fannings" — the broken scraps left after the whole leaves are sorted out — which brew fast, harsh and one-dimensional. Whole loose leaf has room to unfurl and give up its flavour in layers, and it can usually be brewed more than once. There are good teabags and we stock a few, but gram for gram, whole leaf gives you more tea, more character and better value. Come and taste the same garden both ways; the difference is not subtle.

How do I brew tea properly at home?

Three things matter: water temperature, quantity of leaf, and time. Black teas want water just off the boil; greens and whites want it cooler (around 70–80°C) or they scald and turn bitter; oolongs sit in between. Use a generous scoop of leaf and give it room — a basket or pot, not a strangled bag — and time it rather than guessing. We write the numbers on every bag we sell and we're always happy to talk you through a specific tea. Get those three right and even a modest tea tastes good.

What does "single garden" or "single estate" mean?

It means the tea comes from one named farm or estate, and often from one picking or "flush", rather than being blended from many sources to hit a consistent flavour. Single-garden teas taste of their place and their season — a first-flush Darjeeling tastes different from a second-flush from the same estate — which is the whole pleasure of them. Blends have their place (our breakfast blend is built for milk and mornings), but a single garden is where you taste what tea actually is.

Do you have caffeine-free options that are actually good?

Yes — our herbal and tisane range is made from whole botanicals rather than tea-dust with flavour sprayed on, so a peppermint actually tastes of peppermint and a rooibos has body rather than being coloured water. We stock chamomile, peppermint, rooibos, fruit infusions and seasonal blends, all caffeine-free and all tasted the same way we taste our teas. Tell us whether you want something calming, something fruity or something to replace an after-dinner coffee and we'll point you right.

Can I book a tasting, and what happens?

Yes — tastings run in the room upstairs and are one of the nicest ways to get into tea. A session is usually a guided flight of several teas — a single origin explored in depth, a tour of a region, or a beginner's run across black, green, oolong and white — with someone talking you through what you're tasting and how it was made, without the pretension. We run open sessions and private bookings for birthdays, gifts and groups, and gift vouchers are available. It's informal, generous and there's always more tea in the pot.

Do you supply cafés and restaurants?

Gladly — it's a growing part of what we do. We supply loose leaf, service kit and, crucially, staff training, because tea as good as your coffee only works if the team knows how to brew it. We'll help you build a list that suits your menu and your customers, keep you stocked on a standing order, and come back to re-train as staff change. Give us a call and we'll talk through what your place needs rather than sell you a one-size box.
