PLATE 01 / THE LECHONANCEBU, THE OLD WAY

No sauce on the table. If it needs sauce, we roasted it wrong.

Crackle & Coal is a Cebu lechonan, and in Cebu that word carries weight — this is the island that made lechon famous, where the pig is stuffed and seasoned so well it's eaten plain, skin and all, no liver sauce in sight. We roast native pigs whole over coconut-husk coals, the old way, turning the pole by hand for hours until the fat renders clean and the skin turns to glass.

We started in 2011 with one roasting pit behind the family house and a queue that formed before the coals were even lit. The seasoning is our lola's: lemongrass crushed into the cavity, spring onion, garlic, black pepper and a native blend we don't write down, rubbed inside so the flavour comes from within rather than a bottle poured on after. A good Cebu lechon is a whole eaten thing, not a dressed one.

Today we roast for fiestas and weddings across Metro Cebu, sell lechon belly and by-the-kilo for the smaller gatherings, and still light the first coals before dawn on fiesta days. Order a whole one and we'll deliver it hot, skin intact, carried flat so the crackling arrives unbroken — because the crack of that first cut is half the celebration.

Why is Cebu lechon eaten without sauce?

Because a proper Cebu lechon is seasoned from the inside, so the flavour is already in the meat and skin rather than poured on afterwards. When we stuff the cavity with lemongrass, spring onion, garlic, black pepper and our native blend and roast it slowly over coals, that seasoning works through the whole pig as the fat renders — the result is savoury, aromatic and complete on its own. In Manila and much of the country, lechon is served with a sweet liver sauce, and there's nothing wrong with that; it's a different, delicious tradition. But in Cebu the point of pride is that you don't need it. We'll always send a dip along for guests who want one, but if you find yourself reaching for it, tell us — because a Cebu lechon that needs sauce is a lechon we roasted wrong.

How do I know what size to order?

As a rough guide, most people plan for around 250 to 350 grams of lechon per guest at a meal where it's the main dish, a little less if there's a full spread of other food. A small 8-to-10-kilo native pig comfortably feeds a family gathering; a 20-kilo pig suits a big fiesta table; and for weddings and large events we'll often roast two or more so there's carving-station flow and hot pig throughout. If you're not sure, just tell us the headcount and the kind of event and we'll size it for you — we'd far rather you had a little left over (it makes excellent paksiw) than run short in front of your guests. For smaller numbers, lechon belly or by-the-kilo is often the smarter, more economical choice.

Can you deliver, and will the skin still be crisp?

Yes — we deliver hot across Metro Cebu, and keeping the crackling intact is something we take seriously because it's the whole reason you order a lechon. We carry every whole pig flat rather than upright, roast it to time so it comes off the coals close to your serving window, and transport it hot and covered so it doesn't sit and steam its own skin soft. For the crispest result, plan to serve it within an hour or so of delivery and don't cover it tightly once it arrives — trapped steam is the enemy of crackling. If your venue is further out or your timing is tight, tell us and we'll plan the roast and route around it. We can also carve on site for weddings and big events so the pig stays whole and hot until the moment it's served.

What's the difference between whole lechon and lechon belly?

Whole lechon is exactly that — a whole native pig, stuffed, seasoned and roasted over coals, the traditional fiesta centrepiece with the full range of cuts, plenty of crackling and real theatre when it's carried in and carved. Lechon belly is a boneless roll of seasoned pork belly roasted to the same glass-skin crackling, and it's the smarter choice when you don't need a whole pig: there's no bone, it carves into neat slices, every bit is edible meat-and-skin, and it suits offices, smaller family meals and anywhere you want the lechon experience without a whole beast. Both get the same lemongrass-and-native-spice seasoning and the same slow coal roast — the belly is simply the concentrated, no-waste version.

Do you use native pigs, and does it matter?

We use native and free-range pigs wherever we can, sourced whole from farms we know across Cebu and Bohol, and yes, it genuinely matters. Native pigs are smaller, leaner in the right places and firmer in texture, with skin that crackles beautifully and meat that carries seasoning well — they were bred for exactly this. Commercial pigs can be roasted well too, and for very large orders we sometimes need them for size, but for flavour and skin the native pig is the benchmark, which is why we build our relationships with the farms rather than buying blind at market. A great lechon starts days before the coals are lit, with the right pig raised the right way.

How far ahead should I order, especially for fiestas and weddings?

For everyday whole lechon, belly or by-the-kilo, a day or two's notice is usually plenty. For weddings, big fiestas and the fiesta season itself, order as far ahead as you can — a week or two at minimum, and more during peak dates like town fiestas, Christmas and New Year, when the roasting schedule fills quickly and the best native pigs get spoken for. Booking early lets us secure the right pig, plan the roast so it comes off the coals at the perfect time, and arrange carving and delivery without a scramble. And if you're in a genuine last-minute bind, call anyway; sometimes we can fit you in.
