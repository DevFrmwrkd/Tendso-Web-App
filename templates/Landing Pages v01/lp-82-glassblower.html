GATHER 01 / THE STUDIOA GLASS CITY

Glass remembers every second it spends too cool.

We lit the furnace at Furnace & Flask in 2013, on the river that made Sunderland a glass city for three hundred years. We wanted to make hot glass the way it's always been made — gathered from a furnace at eleven hundred degrees, blown and shaped by hand, and annealed slowly so it lives instead of shattering — and to let people stand close enough to feel the heat.

Everything here is one-off or small-run. A gaffer works the piece while an assistant turns and gathers; it's a conversation in molten glass with about a ninety-second window before it cools too far to move. That urgency is the craft. We make vessels, lighting, drinkware and sculpture, and we sign and date what leaves the studio.

We're a teaching studio too. Come for a taster and make your own paperweight or tumbler on the blowpipe with a gaffer beside you — most people arrive nervous and leave grinning, holding something warm they made themselves. Glass looks impossible until you've done it once.

GATHER 03 / THE DIFFERENCEWHY IT'S ONE-OFF

Why it's one-off.

01 / 03

Blown here, watched by you.

Made at our furnace on the Wear, not imported and rebranded. Come and stand by the glory hole while it's being shaped — the heat is part of the experience.

02 / 03

One-off by nature.

Hand-blown glass can't be identical, so we don't pretend. Every piece is signed and dated, and the small variations are the maker's fingerprint.

03 / 03

Annealed properly.

We cool every piece slowly in the annealer overnight so it holds together for years. Rushed glass carries stress and fails; ours is made to last.

GATHER 07 / ASKED AT THE STUDIOBEFORE YOU VISIT

Asked at the studio.

Anything else — ring 0191 565 2200. If we're mid-gather we'll call you back once the piece is in the annealer.

Can I watch glass being blown, and is it free?

Yes — the studio has a viewing area and you're welcome to come and watch a piece being made during working hours, free of charge, as long as we're at the furnace that day. It's genuinely mesmerising and there's real heat coming off the glory hole, so wear something you don't mind being warm in. If you want to be sure we're blowing rather than cold-working or teaching, give us a ring before you set off and we'll tell you what's on that day.

What happens on a taster day?

A taster is roughly two hours at the furnace where, with a gaffer working alongside you, you make your own piece — usually a paperweight, a tumbler or a small bauble depending on the session. You'll gather glass on the blowpipe, shape it, add colour and blow it under guidance; the gaffer handles the genuinely dangerous bits. Because glass has to anneal overnight, you collect or we post your finished piece a few days later. No experience is needed and most people are amazed at what they manage.

Why is hand-blown glass more expensive than shop glass?

Because two skilled people work every piece in real time at a furnace that costs a fortune to run, and because each one is genuinely made by hand rather than pressed out by a machine a thousand at a time. You're paying for the skill, the fuel, the failures that go back in the pot, and a one-off object signed by its maker. Per piece it's more than a supermarket vase; as an object that will outlive you and carry a story, it's very fair.

Can you make lighting to my colour and size?

Yes — lighting and pendant shades are a big part of what we do, for homes and for bars and restaurants. Tell us the colour, the diameter and drop, and how many you need, and we'll blow them as a matched run (matched, not identical — they're hand-made). We can wire pendants to UK spec or supply glass-only for your own electrician to fit. For larger installations give us a realistic lead time and we'll keep you posted as the run comes off the pipe.

Do you repair chipped or broken glass?

Sometimes. Chipped rims can often be ground back and re-polished, and some cracks and losses can be cold-worked or flame-repaired depending on the piece and where the damage is. Shattered glass generally can't be put back together, and we won't pretend otherwise. Bring or send us a photo of the damage and we'll tell you honestly whether it's saveable, what it would cost, and whether it's worth doing — we'd rather be straight than take on a lost cause.

Do you sell gift vouchers?

We do, for taster days and for a value towards anything in the shop, and they're one of our most popular buys — glassblowing is a memorable gift and the finished piece is a keepsake on top of the experience. Vouchers can be bought in the studio or over the phone and posted or emailed. They're dated generously so the recipient can book a session that suits them, and we're happy to help arrange group bookings for birthdays, hen-dos and work outings.
