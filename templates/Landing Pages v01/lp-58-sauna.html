ROUND 01 / THE CLUBPORTOBELLO PROMENADE

Hot fire. Cold sea. Repeat.

The Cold Front is two wood-fired saunas on Portobello prom, a rack of towels, and the entire Firth of Forth as a plunge pool. It started as one barrel sauna and a WhatsApp group of cold-water obsessives; it's now the calmest, reddest-faced club in Edinburgh.

The method is old and simple: heat the body hard in the sauna, cool it fast in the sea, rest, repeat. The contrast is the whole point — the rush at the shoreline, the deep calm after, the slightly evangelical way everyone talks about it in the changing hut.

Sessions are guided but never bossy. First-timers get walked through it; regulars are left to their rounds. The stove is real wood, tended by an actual person, and the sea is whatever temperature the North Sea feels like being. Bracing is a feature.

Why the real thing.

1

Real wood, real sea.

A wood-fired stove tended by hand, not an electric box on a timer, and the actual Firth of Forth, not a plunge tub. The contrast is bigger, the experience is realer.

2

Guided, never bossy.

First-timers are walked through every step; regulars are left to their rounds. Nobody is made to plunge, nobody is made to chat. Your session, your pace.

3

All weather, all year.

The best sessions are the wild ones — sideways rain, four degrees, the sea grey and serious. We run through winter because winter is when this works best.

ROUND 04 / THE ROUNDHEAT → SEA → REST → AGAIN
  1. STEP 01

    Book a slot

    Communal or private, ninety minutes. Bring swimwear, a towel (or hire ours), and a willingness to gasp at the shoreline.

  2. STEP 02

    Heat up

    Into the sauna, 80–90°C, until you're glowing and slightly questioning your choices. Löyly on the stones when you're ready.

  3. STEP 03

    Hit the sea

    Ten steps to the water, in to whatever depth you dare, out when your body insists. The gasp is the point; the calm comes after.

  4. STEP 04

    Rest & repeat

    Wrap up on the bench, let the heat return, go again — two or three rounds is the sweet spot. Leave floaty, warm, and mildly evangelical.

ROUND 07 / ASKED ON THE PROMUSUALLY BEFORE THE FIRST DIP

Asked on the prom.

Anything else — ring 0131 442 5518 or message us. We answer between sessions, damp-handed.

Is the cold water safe?

For most healthy adults, with guidance, yes — and we give it. We brief you on breathing and entry, you go at your own depth and pace, and nobody is ever pushed. If you have a heart condition, are pregnant, or have other health concerns, check with your GP first and tell us at booking.

Do I have to go in the sea?

No. Plenty of people come for the sauna and the calm and dip a toe at most. The contrast is where the magic is, but it's an invitation, not a requirement — cold showers on the prom are a gentler halfway house.

What do I bring?

Swimwear, a towel (or hire one), a water bottle, and something warm for after — a dry robe if you've got one, a big coat if you haven't. We provide the sauna, the löyly water, and somewhere dry to change. Leave the shampoo; this isn't that.

How cold does it actually get?

The Forth runs from about 6°C in winter to 15°C in late summer. Winter is genuinely bracing and, most regulars agree, the best of it. The sauna is 80–90°C, so the contrast is enormous whatever the season.

Can beginners come?

Absolutely — the first-timer sessions exist for exactly this, slower and fully guided. Most people's first dip is far shorter than their fifth, and that's completely normal. You'll know within one round whether you're hooked. You will be.

What if the weather's wild?

Then it's usually a better session. We run in rain, wind and cold; we only cancel for genuine safety reasons — lightning, dangerous surf — and we'll rebook or refund if we do. Dress for the walk down; the fire sorts the rest.
