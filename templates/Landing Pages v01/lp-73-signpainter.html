SIGN 01 / THE TRADEDRAWN FOR THE BOARD

Drawn for the board it's going on.

A hand-painted sign is designed, not selected. We draw the lettering for the actual fascia, the actual trade and the actual town — the weight that reads across the street, the flourish that suits a butcher versus a bookshop, the spacing that a stretched font never gets right. Then we pounce it up and paint it by hand, in enamel that flows into a living edge no plotter-cut vinyl can fake.

Ampersand works out of a lock-up in Hastings Old Town, lettering shopfronts along the south coast and gilding glass for anyone who wants that deep, water-gilded shine behind their window. Gold leaf laid on glass is the oldest trick in the trade and still the most beautiful — it stops passers-by, which is the entire job of a sign.

We letter vans and boats too, and repaint the faded pub signs that have swung over a door for a century. It's a slow craft in a fast world, and every job is a one-off, because your shop isn't the same as the one next door and your sign shouldn't be either.

No vinyl, no plotter, no stretched font. Every letter is drawn for the job and brushed by hand — which is why it has a living edge and why it's still there in thirty years.

02 / 03

Isn't hand-painting more expensive than vinyl?

Up front, usually a bit — you're paying for a skilled person drawing and painting rather than a machine cutting a font. Over the life of the sign it's the cheaper option: vinyl lifts, fades and peels within a few winters and gets replaced, while a properly painted and gilded sign lasts decades and mellows rather than fails. You pay once.

What exactly is gold-leaf glass gilding?

Real gold leaf laid onto the back of glass with a water-and-size technique, so it shows through the window with a deep mirror shine no paint or vinyl can imitate. It's one of the oldest signwriting crafts and still the most striking — it's the lettering that stops people on the pavement. It also lasts extraordinarily well behind glass.

Can you match our brand or a particular style?

Yes — we can work to your existing brand, revive a historic style for a period building, or design lettering from scratch for a new trade. We draw for the specific board and street, so it always looks like it belongs. Bring any brand guidelines or just a sense of the feel and we'll take it from there.

Do you letter vehicles and boats?

We do — vans, trucks and boats, with traditional coachlines, trade lettering and names brushed on by hand. Hand lettering on a vehicle has a quality that print wraps don't, and on a working boat it's the traditional and hard-wearing choice. We'll advise on preparation so it lasts the life of the vehicle.

Can you restore our old or faded sign?

Often, yes — and we'll be honest about whether a sign wants a faithful restoration, a sympathetic revival, or a full repaint. Old pub and shop signs in particular are worth saving where we can. Send us photos and we'll tell you what's possible before committing you to anything.

How long does a sign take?

A shopfront fascia is typically drawn, approved and painted over a couple of weeks depending on weather and the queue; gilding and larger jobs take longer. Weather matters for on-site painting, so we build in sensible timing. If you've an opening date, tell us early and we'll plan the work around it.
