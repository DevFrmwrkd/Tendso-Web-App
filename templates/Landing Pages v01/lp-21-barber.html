01 / THE SHOPRAEBURN PLACE SINCE 1968

Third generation. Same four chairs.

The shop has been on Raeburn Place for fifty-odd years and re-covered the chairs exactly twice. Otto trained his son, his son trained his son, and between them they've cut half of Stockbridge — school photos, weddings, job interviews, and the haircuts in between.

It's scissors first here. Clippers are a tool, not a personality. Every cut ends with a hot towel and a proper check of the neckline, because the back of your head is the part everyone else sees.

Four barbers, four chairs, one kettle. Walk in and take a numbered ticket, or book ahead and skip the bench.

07 / ASKED AT THE BENCHSEVEN ANSWERS

Before you take a ticket.

Anything else — ring 0131 332 4471. Whoever's between cuts picks up.

Do I need to book?

No — the ticket system has run since 1968 and it works. But Saturdays fill by ten, so if your weekend has a schedule, book a chair online.

Can I ask for a specific barber?

Please do. Book by chair number and you'll get the same barber every visit. Your cut card stays with them.

How long does a cut take?

Twenty-five minutes for a scissor cut, thirty-five for a fade, forty for the full hot-towel shave. We don't double-book chairs.

What's a cut card?

A record card your barber keeps — guard lengths, parting, how your crown behaves. Started as Otto's memory aid in the 70s; now it's the system.

Do you cut children's hair?

Under-12s on weekday afternoons, when the shop is calm. First haircuts get a certificate and the lock of hair in an envelope, if you want it.

Do you do house calls?

For clients who can't get to the shop any more — yes, quietly, on Mondays. Ring us and ask for Davie.

Card or cash?

Both, and the card machine doesn't ask you for a tip. The jar on the counter is for the apprentice's kit fund.
