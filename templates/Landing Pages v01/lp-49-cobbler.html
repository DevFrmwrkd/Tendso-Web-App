STUB 01 / THE SHOPSADDLER STREET, APTLY

The bench in the window.

Heel & Last opened in 1978 when Jim Aldred put his bench in the window of a shop on Saddler Street — on the theory, correct for forty-seven years now, that people trust a repair they can watch. His daughter runs the bench today; his grandson runs the stitching machine and the socials, in that order of importance.

The work hasn't changed: rip it back to what's sound, rebuild with materials at least as good as the factory used, and hand it back better balanced than it arrived. Oak-bark-tanned leather for the soles that earn it, proper rubber for the weather this city actually has.

The window bench also produces the shop's favourite genre of moment: someone collecting shoes they'd written off, turning them over twice, and asking if we've swapped them for new ones. We have not. That's the job.

Why repair wins.

1

Repair over replace, on principle.

A £180 resole on £400 shoes beats £400 of new shoes every time — better leather, broken in, and yours. We'll tell you when that maths fails, too.

STUB 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERSHOES IN HAND

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 0191 384 2217. If the stitcher's running we'll hear you second try.

Are my shoes worth repairing?

Bring them in for the free verdict. Rule of thumb: welted or stitched construction, yes, almost always; glued fashion shoes, sometimes; anything with a cardboard midsole, we'll say so kindly and tell you what to buy instead.

How long does a resole take?

Heels while you wait or same day. Full resoles about a week; welted work up to two, because the welt sets the pace and we don't rush welts. The ticket carries a real date.

How much will it cost?

Quoted on sight, written on the ticket, honoured to the penny. Heels are a coffee-and-cake sum; full welted resoles are a serious-shoes sum. Either way it's a fraction of replacing anything worth repairing.

Can you save suede / rain damage / dog incidents?

Usually. Suede revives better than people fear, salt lines lift, and we have seen and reversed every category of dog incident. The only true fatalities are cracked-through soles on glued construction — and even then, ask.

Do you do while-you-wait?

Heels, taps, stretching tweaks and keys, yes — there's a chair and the paper. Bigger jobs deserve bench time; the window seat is for watching, not waiting.

Why should I buy welted shoes?

Because they're the last shoes you'll need to buy — everything on them can be renewed from this bench, for decades. Come in and we'll show you on a cutaway shoe what your money actually buys. No brand allegiance; construction is the religion here.
