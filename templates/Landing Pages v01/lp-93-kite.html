LINE 01 / THE SCHOOLBIG SKY, STEADY WIND

The wind is free. Everything else, we can help you with.

Windward is a kite shop and kite-surf school on the north Norfolk coast, and we're here for a simple reason: this stretch of beach, with its huge skies, wide flat sands and steady onshore wind, is one of the best places in the country to fly anything on a line. We opened in 2011 selling kites off a rack and teaching a handful of lessons; now we're the shop the whole coast comes to, but the spirit's unchanged — get people flying and keep them safe doing it.

We stock the full spread: single-line kites for children and beginners, delta and box kites, precision dual- and quad-line stunt kites, power kites and traction kites, and full kite-surf gear — kites, bars, boards, harnesses, wetsuits. We only sell kit we'd fly ourselves, we rig it and check it before it leaves, and we'll always match the kite to the flyer and the conditions rather than sell you more kite than you can handle, which is how people get hurt or put off.

But the heart of Windward is teaching. Our kite-surf school runs BKSA-recognised lessons from complete-beginner land sessions through to independent riding on the water, taught by qualified instructors with all the safety kit and years of reading this particular beach and its wind and tides. Whether you want a fun afternoon on a power kite or a whole new sport, we'll get you there in the right order, at the right pace, without the scares. The wind's already here; let us do the rest.

LINE 07 / BEFORE YOU FLYGETTING STARTED SAFELY

Before you fly.

Anything else — ring 01485 555 306. We'll always give you a straight answer about the wind and your level.

I've never flown a kite properly — where do I start?

Wherever you are, we'll start you in the right place, and there's no such thing as too much of a beginner here. If you just want the joy of flying, a good single-line or delta kite and ten minutes of coaching on the beach will have you up and grinning; if you fancy control and tricks, a dual-line stunt kite is the next step and enormously satisfying to master. If your eye is on the power kites or kite-surfing, we always, always start on the sand — learning to control a kite in the "wind window", understanding the safety systems, and building real feel before there's any question of being pulled along or getting on the water. The single biggest mistake people make is buying or borrowing too much kite and scaring themselves (or worse) on day one. Come and talk to us, tell us the dream, and we'll set you off on the version of it that starts safely and stays fun.

Is kite-surfing dangerous, and how long does it take to learn?

Kite-surfing done badly is genuinely dangerous — a powered kite is a serious piece of kit — but learned properly, in the right order, with instruction and the modern safety systems, it's a controlled and hugely rewarding sport, which is exactly why we insist on teaching it rather than just selling the gear. Our BKSA-recognised lessons progress through clear stages: kite control and safety on land, body-dragging in the water to recover your board, then water starts and your first rides. Most people need somewhere around six to twelve hours of tuition, typically over several sessions, to reach independent riding — but it varies a lot with the individual, the wind and how often you can get out. We'd rather take you at the pace that keeps you safe and confident than rush you to a certificate; the riders who last are the ones who built solid foundations. Rushing it is how people get hurt or quit.

What kite should I buy for my child?

For a child, the goal is instant success and zero frustration, so we steer firmly towards a good-quality single-line kite — a classic diamond, a delta, or a sled kite — that flies easily and stably in a moderate breeze, rather than anything with two lines or real pull. A well-made beginner kite that actually gets airborne first time is worth ten times a cheap toy-shop kite that nose-dives and ends the afternoon in tears; the difference in flying quality is enormous and the price difference is small. We'll match it to your child's age and size, show them (and you) how to launch and fly it on the beach before you leave, and tell you honestly what wind it needs. As they get keener, dual-line stunt kites are a brilliant next step, but there's no rush — plenty of adults are perfectly happy flying a single-liner on a summer beach forever, and quite right too.

Do I need my own gear for lessons, or is it provided?

Everything you need for lessons is provided and included — kites, bars, boards, harnesses, helmets, buoyancy aids and wetsuits — so you can come and learn without spending a penny on kit first, which is exactly how it should be. There's no sense buying gear before you know the sport suits you and before you understand, through learning, what kind of kit you'll actually want; we've seen too many people buy an expensive setup that turns out to be wrong for them. Just bring swimwear to go under the wetsuit, a towel, and something warm for afterwards. Once you're riding independently and know what you like, we'll help you buy your own gear, sized and chosen properly for you and this coast — and until then, you're welcome to hire from us. Learn first, buy later, ride happy.

When's the best time and wind to come, and what if it's not windy?

Kites need wind, and the honest truth is that some days simply don't deliver — but this coast is blessed with reliable onshore breezes, and we plan around the forecast so you're rarely disappointed. For lessons, we book you onto sessions when the wind is forecast to be in the right range (too little and nothing flies; too much and it's unsafe for learning), and if conditions don't materialise we'll reschedule you rather than send you out in the wrong wind — a lesson in no wind teaches nothing and a lesson in too much wind is dangerous. For casual kite-flying, spring through autumn on a breezy day is glorious, and we're always happy to tell you over the phone whether it's worth the trip. The beauty of the big Norfolk sky is that there's usually a good wind somewhere in the week; check with us, stay a little flexible, and we'll get you flying.

Do you repair kites, and can you fix any kind?

Yes — repairs are a real part of what we do, and we can mend the great majority of kites and kite-surf gear rather than see them thrown away. Ripped and torn canopies, broken or bent spars and frames, worn, snapped or tangled flying lines, bridles that need re-tuning, harness and bar wear — bring it in and we'll assess it honestly, tell you what it'll cost, and be straight if a kite is genuinely beyond economic repair (which is rare). A good kite is expensive and a well-made one is worth keeping alive for many seasons; a canopy repair or a new set of lines often costs a fraction of replacement and restores it to full performance. We stock spares for the kit we sell and can source parts for much of what we don't. Whether it's a child's beach kite or a high-end kite-surf canopy, if it can fly again, we'll do our best to get it there.
