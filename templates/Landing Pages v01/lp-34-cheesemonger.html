WHEEL 01 / THE SHOPA SHOP ABOVE, A CELLAR BELOW

A shop above, a cellar below.

Rennet & Rind started as a market stall with eight cheeses and a fridge that worried everyone. The shop came in 2014; the cellar — the point of the whole exercise — in 2016. It sits under the shopfloor at eleven degrees and ninety per cent humidity, and it is where cheeses arrive young and leave ready.

Maturing our own stock means we're not selling what a wholesaler decided was ready. A Lincolnshire Poacher might sit down there for four extra months. A Baron Bigod leaves the moment its centre goes glossy. The cellar decides; we just turn the wheels.

There are two of us behind the counter and neither of us will sell you a cheese we wouldn't take home. Some days that means talking you out of the famous one and into the better one.

WHEEL 03 / THE POINTTHREE THINGS WE'D PUT ON A PLAQUE

Why a cellar matters.

I of III

We mature it ourselves.

The cellar under the shop holds every wheel until it's ready. What you taste is a decision, not a delivery date.

II of III

Taste before you buy.

Every cheese, every visit, no purchase necessary. A cheese that can't survive being tasted shouldn't be on the counter.

III of III

Named farms, named makers.

Every label carries the farm, the maker and the milk. If we can't tell you who made it and when, we don't stock it.

WHEEL 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERUSUALLY MID-SLIVER

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 01223 315 993. If we're mid-cut we'll ring back once the wedge is wrapped.

Can I really taste anything?

Anything on the counter, yes. Cheeses in the cellar aren't ready — that's why they're in the cellar — but ask and we'll tell you when they surface.

How much cheese do I need for a board?

About 80–100g per person if there's other food; 150g if the cheese is the event. Three to five cheeses beats seven — a board should be an argument, not a crowd.

Do you deliver?

In Cambridge, yes, by bike trailer, usually next day. Further afield we'd rather you found your good local monger — but the subscription posts nationally in insulated boxes, reluctantly.

How should I store cheese at home?

Waxed paper, the salad drawer, and out of the fridge an hour before eating. Cling film is how good cheese dies; we will re-wrap anything you bring back in shame, free of charge.

I'm pregnant — what can I have?

Plenty. Hard cheeses are fine, as are thermised and many pasteurised soft ones. Every label on our counter states the milk treatment, and we keep an up-to-date NHS-guidance list behind the counter.

Do you do cheese wedding cakes?

Stacked wheels, sized to your headcount, delivered and assembled. Book eight weeks ahead — the right wheels need time in the cellar, and the cellar will not be rushed.

Vegetarian rennet?

Lots of our list, clearly marked. Traditional rennet cheeses are marked too. Ask and we'll steer you — some of the best vegetarian-rennet cheeses in Britain are within an hour of this shop.
