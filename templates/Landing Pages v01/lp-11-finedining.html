The room

A room the size of the idea.

Corvid opened in 2019 in a silk-weaver's atelier halfway up the Croix-Rousse hill — one long room, a counter of sixteen seats, and a kitchen you can hear breathing. The name is the crow's family: birds that remember faces and hold grudges. So do chefs.

The menu is written every Sunday for the week ahead, from what the Monts du Lyonnais and the Saône valley are actually producing. It is served to everyone, together, in one seating.

The wine list is one page. The music is on vinyl. The night ends when the last table stands up, and no earlier.

What the house offers

The evening, in six parts.

The tasting menu

Nine to eleven courses, written weekly. Served to the whole room at one seating, Tuesday to Saturday.

The vegetal menu

The same night, the same rhythm, no animal at the centre of any plate. Written with equal seriousness.

Wine pairing

Six pours from a one-page list — Beaujolais, Jura, Northern Rhône, and whatever the sommelier drove back from on Monday.

The counter

Four of the sixteen seats face the pass. Same menu, closest heat. Requested at reservation, granted by luck.

Whole-room evenings

The entire room, sixteen guests, your occasion. One menu, written for the date. Two months' notice.

The archive

Every menu since opening night, printed and bound by year. Read them in the entryway while your coat dries.

Why it works this way

Three convictions, held nightly.

i.

One seating, one rhythm.

Everyone eats together. The kitchen cooks each course once, at its exact moment — nothing waits under a lamp.

ii.

The menu is written weekly.

Sunday afternoon, from what the valley actually has. No signature dishes, no greatest hits, no permanent anything.

iii.

Sixteen seats is the whole restaurant.

No second room, no private annexe, no expansion plan. The size is the point.

How a night unfolds

From the first of the month to the last candle.

  1. I.

    Reservations open monthly

    On the first of each month, at noon, for the month following. By the form or by phone.

  2. II.

    Tell us everything

    Allergies, aversions, the vegetal menu, the occasion. The menu bends before you arrive, not at the table.

  3. III.

    Arrive at eight

    The room seats together. The first course leaves the pass when the last coat is hung.

  4. IV.

    Stay as long as the night

    There is no second seating. Coffee is included. So is the argument about dessert.

Asked, answered

Before you book.

How do reservations work?

They open on the first of each month at noon, for the following month, through the form or by phone. The room is sixteen seats, so most nights fill within the week. Cancellations go to the waiting list in order.

Can you accommodate allergies and aversions?

Yes — tell us at reservation, not at the table. The menu is adjusted for you before the night begins. Severe allergies are taken seriously; we will call you to talk it through.

Is there a vegetarian or vegan menu?

The vegetal menu runs every night, written with the same seriousness as the main menu. Vegan on request at reservation.

What does dinner cost?

The menu price is fixed and stated on the reservation form for the month you book. Pairings are optional and priced alongside. We have no hidden supplements.

Can we bring children?

Children who can enjoy a three-hour dinner are welcome; there is a shorter menu for them. Most families choose Tuesday, the gentlest night.

Is there a dress code?

No. The room is warm, the floor is old, the dog next door doesn't judge and neither do we.

Can we book the whole room?

Yes — sixteen guests, one menu written for your date, two months' notice. Write to the table address.

Where do we park?

You mostly don't, this high on the hill. The Croix-Rousse métro is four minutes' walk; the C13 stops at the corner.
