Suki Fresh is a fish-and-meat stall in Pasig Public Market — the palengke, the beating heart of how Filipinos have always shopped for food. A palengke isn't a supermarket; it's rows of family vendors, haggling and banter, produce that arrived hours ago, and the all-important suki relationship: the loyal-customer bond where your vendor knows your name, your usual order, sets aside the best cut for you, and gives you a fair price because you keep coming back. That trust is the whole system, and it's ours.

What is a palengke, and what does 'suki' mean?

A palengke is a Filipino public wet market — rows of independent family-run stalls selling fresh fish, meat, vegetables, fruit and everything for the kitchen, and it's how most Filipinos have traditionally shopped for food. Unlike a supermarket, a palengke runs on relationships, freshness and a bit of friendly haggling; produce typically arrives that same morning and turns over fast. 'Suki' is the heart of it: the Filipino term for the loyal-customer / trusted-vendor relationship. When you're someone's suki, they know you, they know your usual order, they set aside the best pieces for you, they give you a fair suki price without you having to haggle hard, and they'll tell you honestly what's freshest and what to skip. In return, you keep coming back. It's a bond built on mutual trust over time, and we've built our whole stall on being a suki you can rely on.

How do I know the fish is actually fresh?

There are clear physical signs, and part of a good vendor's job is to point them out honestly. In truly fresh fish, the eyes are clear, bright and slightly bulging (not sunken, cloudy or grey), the gills are bright red or pink (not brown or slimy), the flesh is firm and springs back when pressed, and the smell is clean and of the sea. Fresh fish also has bright, intact scales and shiny skin. At our stall everything comes in before dawn and goes straight onto ice, and we're happy for you to look closely — we'll show you the eyes and gills ourselves. For delivery customers, this is exactly why the suki relationship matters: you're relying on us to apply those standards on your behalf and only send you fish we'd cook for our own family. If anything we deliver ever isn't right, tell us — a suki relationship works both ways.

Do you really clean and cut everything for free?

Yes — free cleaning and preparation is a standard, expected part of good palengke service, not an extra. For fish, that means scaling, gutting, removing gills, and slicing or filleting however your recipe needs — whole, butterflied, in steaks, deboned where possible. For meat, it means trimming, slicing, cubing or grinding your pork, chicken or beef to suit the dish, and parting a whole chicken. Just tell us what you're cooking — sinigang, adobo, ginataan, nilaga, caldereta — and we'll prepare the fish or cut to match, because the right prep makes the dish easier and better. It also keeps your kitchen clean. For delivery orders we do all of this before packing, so everything arrives cleaned, cut and ready for the pot.

How does the morning fresh list and ordering work?

It's the modern version of what the palengke always did informally. Each morning, once our fish is in and on ice and we know what came best from the boats and the port, and which meat cuts we have, we message our suki list the day's fresh selection, often with prices and a note on what's especially good. You just reply — by chat or a quick call — with what you'd like and, ideally, how you plan to cook it, so we can pick the right item and prep it accordingly. We confirm your order and total, clean and prepare everything, and either set it aside for pickup or send it out for same-day delivery around Pasig. To join, just message us. There's no obligation to order every day, and many regulars set a standing order we simply prepare and deliver without them having to think about it.

What areas do you deliver to, and when?

We deliver around Pasig and the immediately surrounding areas, same-day, for orders placed in the morning — the point is to get you genuinely fresh palengke fish and meat while it's at its best, so the window is built around the morning's arrivals. Order early and we'll get it to you that day, cleaned, prepped and packed cold. Exact coverage and cut-off times vary a little with the day and demand, so message us your location and we'll tell you straight whether we can reach you and by when. If you're just outside our usual range, ask anyway — depending on the day and your order we can sometimes work something out, especially for standing orders. Anyone near Pasig Public Market is always welcome at the table too.

Is buying from a palengke stall as safe and good as a supermarket?

In our honest view, for fish and meat a good palengke stall is often fresher than a supermarket, precisely because the produce arrives that morning, turns over quickly, and comes from a vendor whose livelihood depends on repeat customers trusting the quality. Supermarket trays can be fine, but may have been packed a day or more earlier, and you can't inspect the fish's eyes and gills through shrink-wrap. The one thing a supermarket offers is standardised handling, so the fair concern with a palengke is hygiene and cold-chain — and that's exactly where choosing a reputable stall matters. We keep our fish properly iced, our meat delivered fresh daily and kept cold, our table and tools clean, and for delivery we pack everything cold and hygienically. The suki relationship is itself a safeguard: a vendor who wants you back for another twenty years cannot afford to make you sick.
