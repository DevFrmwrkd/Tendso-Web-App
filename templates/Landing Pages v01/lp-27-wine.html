I / THE HOUSEMEDIEVAL UPSTAIRS, VAULTED BELOW

A shop upstairs. A cellar below.

Bin Twelve sits in a medieval building on Fossgate with a barrel-vaulted cellar the previous tenants used for storage. We use it for the important things: ageing the wines that deserve it, and pouring for anyone who comes down on a Friday evening.

The list is four hundred wines, almost all from growers rather than négociants. We taste everything before it goes on a shelf, and we drop wines the moment a vintage disappoints, whatever the margin was.

The house rule is that the £9 bottle gets the same straight answer as the £90 one. Wine has enough gatekeepers; we'd rather be the doorman who waves you through.

III / THE HOUSE RULESTHREE OF THEM

House rules.

I.

Tasted before stocked.

Nothing goes on a shelf on reputation alone. Every wine here has been through the tasting bench upstairs — and plenty haven't made it past.

II.

The £9 rule.

The cheap-bottle question gets our full attention. Anyone can sell a great £50 Burgundy; finding the honest £9 Tuesday wine is the real job.

III.

Growers, named.

The label card on every shelf says who farmed it, where, and how. If we can't tell you that, we don't stock it.

VII / ASKED AT THE COUNTERWITHOUT JUDGEMENT

Ask us anything.

Truly anything — ring 01904 655 212. "What goes with fish fingers" has been asked and answered well.

I don't know anything about wine. Is that awkward?

It's our favourite kind of customer. Say what you like drinking — even if the answer is "the pink one from the supermarket" — and we'll take it from there without a single raised eyebrow.

How do the Friday tastings work?

Six wines, one theme, 7pm in the cellar, about twenty people. Tickets are on the website; the price comes off any purchase that evening. Themes range from "proper Chianti" to "wines for people who say they hate Riesling".

Do you deliver?

Free in York by cargo bike, usually same day. Yorkshire-wide in the van on Thursdays. Further afield by courier, packed to survive anything short of deliberate malice.

Can you find a specific bottle?

Often. We have good lines to importers and growers, and a want-book behind the counter. If it's findable at a sane price we'll find it; if the price is silly, we'll say so before you commit.

What if I don't like a bottle you recommended?

Bring it back — even opened. If we guessed wrong about your taste, that's our error, and the replacement is on us. This costs us almost nothing per year because we listen the first time.

Do you buy wine?

Cellars and collections, yes — valued honestly against current market, not against hope. Anything with provenance gaps we'll decline and explain why.

Sale-or-return for parties?

By the unopened case, yes, with free glass hire. Order generously; you only pay for what gets drunk, and running dry at a wedding is a memory that lasts.
