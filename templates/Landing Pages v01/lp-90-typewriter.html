KEY 01 / THE BENCHWORKING MACHINES, NOT PROPS

We don't sell nostalgia. We sell working machines that happen to be beautiful.

Ribbon & Return is a typewriter shop and repair bench in Edinburgh, run by people who use these machines every day and can't stand seeing a good one sold as a dusty prop. We started in 2009 fixing our own collection and friends' finds, and it grew into a shop because it turned out half the city had a grandmother's Olivetti in a cupboard that only needed a service to sing again.

Every typewriter we sell is a genuine restoration, not a wipe-down and a price tag: stripped, degreased, re-lubricated with the right oils in the right places, segment cleaned, type slugs de-gunked, platen assessed, alignment and escapement adjusted, and then typed hard, page after page, until we'd happily use it ourselves. Then it gets a fresh ribbon, a guarantee, and a promise of ribbons for life.

We service and repair machines you already own, sell restored ones across the whole span from portable manuals to grand electrics, stock ribbons and supplies for models the big shops gave up on decades ago, and hire typewriters out to film and TV productions, weddings and events. Whatever brings you in — a writer's tool, a gift, a prop, or a cupboard rescue — you'll leave with something that works.

Should I buy a manual or an electric typewriter?

It depends entirely on how and where you'll use it, and we'll help you feel the difference rather than guess. Manual typewriters need no power, go anywhere, are simpler to maintain and give you that direct, physical connection to the page that most people are really after — the trade-off is that they need a firmer, more even touch and can tire your hands over very long sessions. Electric machines are lighter to the touch, give a crisp consistent impression and suit long typing days, but they need mains power, are heavier, more complex inside, and a bit less romantic. For most people writing at home we steer them to a good manual portable; for someone typing all day, or with a lighter touch, an electric can be kinder. Come and type on both — ten minutes at the keys tells you more than any spec sheet.

Is it hard to get ribbons, and will my machine become unusable?

This is the single biggest worry people have and the single biggest reason old typewriters end up dead in cupboards — so we've built the shop around solving it. The good news is that the vast majority of typewriters, even quite obscure ones, take ribbon on standard spools that we stock or can wind fresh ribbon onto for you, so "they don't make ribbons any more" is almost never actually true. When you buy a machine from us, we tell you exactly what it takes and keep you supplied for as long as we're open — ribbons for life is a genuine promise, not a slogan. If you already own a machine and can't find ribbon, bring it in; we'll identify what it needs and sort you out. A typewriter should never die for want of a ribbon.

How much does a good restored typewriter cost?

Less than people fear for a machine that'll outlive them, and it spans a wide range. A solid, fully-restored manual portable — a great everyday writer's machine — sits in the middle of our range; simpler or more basic models cost less, while sought-after classics, grand desk machines and pristine rare models cost more. What you're paying for above the raw machine is the restoration: hours of stripping, cleaning, adjusting and hard testing, plus a fresh ribbon, a guarantee and ribbons for life. A cheap untouched typewriter off an auction site might cost less upfront and then need all of that work anyway, often more than the machine is worth. We'll always show you options at different prices and be honest about which represent good value to actually use, not just to look at.

My old family typewriter is seized/filthy — is it worth saving?

Nearly always, yes, and reviving family machines is some of the work we love most. A typewriter that's "seized" has usually just got decades of hardened old oil, dust and grime gumming up mechanisms that are themselves perfectly sound — strip it, degrease it, re-lubricate it properly, and it comes back to life astonishingly often. Genuinely dead machines, with cracked frames, badly rusted parts or a perished platen we can't replace, do exist but are the exception. Bring it in and we'll assess it honestly and for free: tell you what's wrong, what a service or restoration would cost, and whether it's worth it — factoring in that a machine with family history is worth saving at a point where a random junk-shop find might not be. We'll never charge you to fix something we'd tell you to walk away from.

Can I hire typewriters for a film, wedding or event?

Yes — hire is a big part of what we do, from single machines to whole period-correct sets. For film, TV and theatre we supply working, era-appropriate typewriters (a 1920s office and a 1970s newsroom used very different machines, and we care about getting it right), delivered with ribbons, spares and, if you need it, someone on set who knows how they were actually used. For weddings and events, a restored typewriter makes a wonderful guestbook or centrepiece — guests type their messages, and it's far more memorable than a card. We handle delivery, setup, paper and ribbons, and collection afterwards. Tell us the date, the era and the scale, and we'll put a package together.

Do you buy typewriters, or take them in part-exchange?

Both, depending on the machine. We're always interested in good typewriters, whether a single quality machine, a collection, or something unusual, and we'll give you an honest assessment — what it is, its condition, and what we can offer, whether outright or against something you're buying from us. We're particularly keen on well-kept classics and less common models, but we'll look at anything and tell you straight if it's better sold elsewhere or simply not worth much. If you've inherited a machine and don't know what it is, send us photographs of the whole typewriter, the make and model names, and the type it produces, and we'll tell you what you've got. Honesty about value is how we've kept the trade's trust since 2009.
