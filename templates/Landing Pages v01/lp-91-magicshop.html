ACT 01 / THE SHOPTHE DEMONSTRATION COUNTER

Anyone can sell you a gimmick. We sell you the effect — and how to actually do it.

Smoke & Mirrors is a magic shop in the Brighton Lanes, run by working magicians who've spent their lives at the craft. We opened in 1994 as the antidote to the sad little joke-shop magic set: a real shop with a demonstration counter, where you see the effect performed properly before you part with a penny, and where the person serving you can actually do the trick.

We stock close-up magic — cards, coins, everyday-object miracles — and stage and parlour material, from a first self-working card trick for a nervous beginner to serious professional apparatus. We carry the good stuff: well-made props, the classic books that teach technique properly, and the effects that reward practice rather than promising miracles with none. And we'll steer you away from the overpriced gadget that does the work for you and dies in a drawer.

But the honest truth of this shop, and we tell everyone who comes in, is that the prop is the easy part. The secret to every piece of magic worth watching is the hours of practice, the misdirection, the patter and the nerve behind it. So we teach — one-to-one lessons for all levels — we recommend the books that matter, and we'll always be straight about what a trick can and can't do. Buy the effect here; earn the wonder at home.

I've never done magic — where should I start?

At the counter, telling us honestly that you're a complete beginner — it's our favourite place to start, and there's no judgement whatsoever. The worst thing a new magician can do is buy a difficult sleight-of-hand effect, fail at it, and conclude they "can't do magic"; the skill was just years away. We'll steer you instead to well-made self-working effects (which are astonishing and need no finger-flinging) and one or two simple techniques, paired with a good beginner's book and, ideally, a lesson to get you going. You'll be genuinely fooling friends within days, building real skills as you go, and enjoying it — which is the whole point. From there the craft opens up as far as you want to take it. Start easy, succeed early, and let the wonder pull you deeper.

Isn't it pointless buying a trick if you'll just look up the secret online?

You can find almost any secret online, and it's the biggest misunderstanding about magic there is — because knowing the secret is not the same as being able to do the trick, not remotely. The secret to most great effects is disappointingly simple; the magic is entirely in the execution: the hours of practice, the misdirection, the timing, the patter, the nerve to perform it under real eyes. Watching a tutorial teaches you the method in five minutes and leaves you utterly unable to fool anyone for months. What you're really buying from us is well-made props that work reliably, the proper teaching that turns a method into a performance, and honest guidance on what's worth your practice time. The knowledge is free; the skill is everything, and that's what we help you build.

Do you actually teach magic, and who are the lessons for?

Yes — one-to-one lessons are, we'd argue, the most valuable thing in the shop, and they're for everyone from nervous first-timers to working pros polishing an act. A lesson with a magician who performs for a living short-cuts months of frustrated solo practice: we'll correct the tiny things that give the game away, teach you misdirection and patter that no tutorial conveys, build a small set of effects you can actually perform with confidence, and be honest about where your practice will pay off. We tailor entirely to you — a child learning their first three tricks, an adult wanting a party piece, a keen hobbyist levelling up, or a semi-pro sharpening their close-up set. Book a single lesson to try it or a series to really progress; either way you'll leave able to do something you couldn't before.

Can you help me choose a gift for someone into magic?

Gladly, and we'll make sure it's something they'll actually treasure rather than a duplicate or a disappointment. Tell us as much as you know — their age, roughly how experienced they are, whether they like cards, coins or bigger effects, and anything they already own — and we'll suggest options across your budget, from a beautifully-made deck or a classic book to a proper effect or a course of lessons. If you're unsure of their level (easy to misjudge), a gift voucher lets them choose with our guidance, which experienced magicians often prefer. For children, we're careful to pick real, sturdy effects that build genuine skill and delight rather than plastic novelties that break and bore. And we gift-wrap. A well-chosen bit of magic is a gift people remember for years.

How do I hire a magician for my wedding or event?

Just tell us about your occasion and we'll match you to the right performer from magicians we know, have vetted, and would happily put our own name to — all insured and experienced. Close-up (or "walk-around") magic is wonderful for drinks receptions and between-course lulls, with a performer moving among your guests doing miracles inches from their eyes; stage or cabaret magic suits a seated audience and a set-piece moment. We'll talk through your numbers, timings, venue and the feel you want, then recommend the right style and performer and handle the booking. Because we're magicians ourselves, we know who's genuinely good with a real audience rather than just polished online, and we won't send you anyone we wouldn't want at our own party. Get in touch well ahead for popular dates.

Do you buy or take in old magic props, books and collections?

Often, yes — we're always interested in quality magic, particularly good books, well-made apparatus and vintage or collectable effects, whether a single item or an entire collection from a magician downsizing or an estate. We'll give you an honest assessment of what things are and what we can offer, outright or on commission through the shop, and we'll tell you frankly if something is better suited to a specialist magic auction. Magic books in particular can be surprisingly valuable and are easy to undervalue if you don't know the field. If you've inherited a magician's effects and have no idea what they are, bring them in or send photographs; we'll happily tell you what you've got, which is sometimes a lovely surprise and always, at least, an interesting afternoon.
