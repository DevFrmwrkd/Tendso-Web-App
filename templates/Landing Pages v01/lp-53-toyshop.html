SHELF 01 / THE SHOPMINUTES OF THE COMMITTEE

A shop with a committee of eleven.

The Marble Jar opened in 2011 with one rule: nothing goes on the shelves until real children have played with it, hard, and it survived — both the toy and the interest. The testing committee is eleven local kids who take the job with terrifying seriousness. Their rejection notes are pinned in the back room. Some are devastating.

The shop runs on marbles, literally: the giant jar on the counter is the loyalty scheme. A marble per visit, a full mini-jar earns the toy of your choice off the WELL DONE shelf. Adults have asked to join. Adults may not join.

The try table is the heart of it: whatever's new sits out, gets played with, gets broken or doesn't, and the committee's verdict card sits beside it. Buy nothing, play anyway. That's what a toy shop is for.

House rules.

1

The committee decides.

Eleven kids, ages 3–12, veto power over every shelf. If it bores them in ten minutes or breaks in five, it doesn't get in. Their standards exceed ours.

2

Play before you pay.

The try table is always out, always free, always covered in whatever's new. A toy that only works in the box isn't a toy; it's packaging.

3

Wrapped free, remembered forever.

Gift wrapping costs nothing, ever, and the birthday book remembers what you bought last year. Small shop memory beats big shop stock.

SHELF 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERUSUALLY MID-WRAP

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 01756 794 233. December answers arrive between ribbon curls.

What age range do you cover?

Nought to about twelve on the shelves, plus a games wall that goes all the way up — the "will Dad actually enjoy this" shelf peaks at adults who claim to be buying for children. No judgement. Some judgement. It's a good shelf.

Can my kid really just come and play?

Yes — the try table exists for exactly that, no purchase expected. Rainy Saturday regulars are a recognised species here. We only ask that big feelings and small pieces both get picked up before you leave.

How does the marble jar work?

One marble per visit into your named mini-jar on the shelf. A full jar (about fifteen visits) earns a pick from the WELL DONE shelf, handed over with ceremony. Marbles are non-transferable; the committee checks.

Is gift wrapping really free?

Always, on anything, in proper paper with curled ribbon. December queues move slower because of it and we consider this the correct trade-off. Tell us if it's a party emergency; we have a fast lane.

Do you do birthday parties or party bags?

Party bags, yes — built to budget with things kids keep, not landfill with a whistle in it. Whole parties, no — but the birthday book service means we'll have the present sorted in four minutes.

What if a toy breaks?

If it broke doing what a toy should do, bring it back — replaced or refunded, no receipt archaeology. Then it goes in the committee's incident log, and believe us, they take a broken toy personally.
