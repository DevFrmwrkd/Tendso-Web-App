TIDE 01 / THE BARA MOUTHFUL OF THE SEA

An oyster is a mouthful of the sea it grew in. Don't drown it.

We opened Brine & Bivalve in 2017 on the harbour where Whitstable has landed oysters since the Romans. We wanted a bar that treats an oyster the way a good wine merchant treats a bottle — graded, named, and served in its best condition, not smothered in sauce to hide that it was opened an hour ago in a back kitchen.

Everything is shucked to order at the bar in front of you. We work with native flats when they're in season — the R-months — and rock oysters year round, from beds we can name along this coast and beyond. We grade by size, keep them cold and cupped-side down, and open them so the liquor stays in the shell where it belongs.

Beyond the oysters there's a raw bar that changes with the boats: clams, cockles, dressed crab, prawns and whatever came in that morning. Cold local beer, crisp wine by the glass, good bread and proper butter. Simple, cold, fresh, and gone in one sitting — the way the sea intended.

Why it tastes of the sea.

01 / 03

Shucked in front of you.

Never pre-opened, never sat on melting ice. An oyster opened to order keeps its liquor and its life; one opened an hour ago has already given up. You watch it happen.

02 / 03

The bed, named.

We tell you where each oyster grew, because that's where its flavour comes from — briny, sweet, coppery or clean depending on the water. Terroir is not just for wine.

03 / 03

Cold, simple, gone.

Great shellfish needs almost nothing: ice, lemon, bread, butter. We resist the urge to dress it to death and let the sea do the talking.

TIDE 04 / HOW IT GOESSIT → PICK → SHUCK → SLURP
  1. STEP 01

    Grab a stool

    Pull up to the bar or a harbour table. Tell us if you're an oyster veteran or trying your first — no judgement either way, everyone starts somewhere.

  2. STEP 02

    Pick your beds

    We'll talk you through what's on ice — native or rock, big or petite, briny or sweet — and build you a half-dozen or a dozen across the beds to compare.

  3. STEP 03

    Watch it shucked

    We open them to order at the bar, liquor kept in the shell, and set them down with mignonette, lemon and a wedge of bread. Cold wine optional but advised.

  4. STEP 04

    Slurp, don't chew

    Tip, sip the liquor, let it go. Then do it again. First-timers get a quick how-to; regulars just get another round and a top-up of the wine.

What's the difference between native and rock oysters?

Native oysters (the flat, round Ostrea edulis) are the traditional British oyster, in season only in the R-months (September to April), with a deep, mineral, almost metallic flavour that oyster lovers prize — and a price to match, because they grow slowly. Rock oysters (the teardrop-shaped Pacific oyster) are available year round, generally milder, meatier and sweeter, and a friendlier place to start. We stock both when we can and we're happy to give you one of each to taste the difference; most people are surprised how distinct they are.

Are oysters safe to eat raw, and when shouldn't I?

Fresh, properly handled oysters kept cold and shucked to order are safe for most people to eat raw, and that's exactly how we serve them. That said, raw shellfish isn't advised for anyone pregnant, very young, elderly, or with a compromised immune system or liver condition — for those we're glad to serve other things from the menu. If an oyster ever smells off or its shell is open and won't close, it doesn't get served; we grade and check every one before it's shucked.

How do I actually eat an oyster?

Loosen it from the shell with the little fork if we haven't already, add a touch of mignonette or a squeeze of lemon if you like (a good oyster needs very little), then bring the shell to your lips, tip it back and let the oyster and its liquor slide in. Give it a chew or two to get the flavour — the "just swallow it" advice sells the oyster short — then finish the liquor. If it's your first, tell us and we'll walk you through it at the bar; nobody's born knowing how.

Can I buy oysters to take home?

Yes — we sell them by the dozen, graded and unopened, packed cold to travel. Keep them in the fridge cupped-side down under a damp cloth, not in water or a sealed bag, and eat them within a day or two while they're at their best. We'll lend you a shucking knife and show you the safe way to open them (a tea towel and a twist, not brute force) so you keep your fingers. If you'd rather we shucked them for a party, we can do that too with a little notice.

Do I need to book, and can you do big groups?

For the bar and harbour tables we take bookings and strongly suggest them at weekends and through the summer, when the stools fill fast — but we always keep a few spaces for walk-ins, so it's worth trying your luck. For groups, parties and celebrations, book ahead and we'll set aside space and make sure there are enough oysters for everyone. For weddings and events we bring a shucking station to you; give us numbers and a date.

Do you supply restaurants and events?

We do — graded native and rock oysters supplied to kitchens along the coast, and full shucking stations for weddings, parties and corporate dos. For trade we'll keep you stocked to order with oysters we've graded and checked ourselves, so what reaches your pass is already sorted. For events we bring the ice, the chiller, the knives and the shuckers, and open to order in front of your guests. Tell us what you need and we'll make it work.
