SHEET 01 / THE SHOPBY THE PRIME MERIDIAN

We sell originals, describe them honestly, and never pass off a reproduction.

Compass & Quarter is an antiquarian map shop a few hundred yards from the Royal Observatory, where the world's meridian is drawn on the ground. We opened in 1998, and in a trade with more than its share of sharp practice, our whole reputation rests on one thing: what we sell is genuine, and described exactly as it is — the date, the mapmaker, the condition, every repair and every blemish.

We deal in original antique maps, sea charts, town plans and celestial prints, from grand engraved wall maps to small county sheets you can hold in one hand. Prices run from a few pounds for a modest print to serious sums for a rare early chart, and we love both ends equally. If you've come for the map of the town you grew up in, or the sea your grandfather sailed, that matters here as much as any collector's piece.

And we don't just sell and wave you off. We frame to conservation standard so a map outlives you rather than fading in the sun, we value for insurance and probate, we advise collectors building a focused collection, and we'll tell you honestly when something you've been offered elsewhere is a reproduction dressed up as the real thing. A good map deals in truth; so do we.

How do I know a map is genuinely antique and not a reproduction?

It's the question the whole trade turns on, and it's exactly why we exist. Genuine antique maps show their age honestly — the paper has a particular feel and tone, engraved maps have a plate mark and ink that sits on the surface in a way modern printing can't fake, and there are the small irregularities of hand-work and hand-colouring. Reproductions, however "aged", tend to give themselves away under a loupe and to a trained eye. We catalogue every map we sell with its date, mapmaker and printing method, we'll show you exactly what we're looking at and why we're confident, and we give a written description you can rely on. Just as importantly, if you bring us something bought elsewhere we'll tell you honestly what it is — we've saved a good many people from an expensive mistake, and occasionally delighted someone who owned a genuine piece without knowing it.

I don't collect — can I still buy a map of somewhere meaningful?

Absolutely, and it's some of our favourite trade. You don't need to be a collector or spend a fortune; many of our loveliest maps are modest county sheets and town plans that cost far less than people expect. Tell us the place that matters — where you grew up, where you married, a coast you love, a city you lived in — and roughly when, and we'll bring out what we have and explain each one. A genuine antique map of a place with meaning, properly framed, is an extraordinarily personal thing to own or to give, and worlds away from a printed poster. We'll take just as much care over a ten-pound print for your kitchen as over a rare chart for a serious collection.

Do you frame maps, and why does "conservation framing" matter?

Yes, framing is a big part of what we do, and it matters more than most people realise. Antique paper and ink are fragile: ordinary framing — acidic mounts, backing boards that off-gas, glass with no UV protection — will yellow, foxing and fade a map within years, sometimes irreversibly. Conservation framing uses acid-free, archival materials, UV-filtering glazing, and reversible mounting techniques that hold the map without glue or tape touching it, so nothing we do can't be safely undone by a future conservator. It costs a little more than a high-street frame and it is the single best thing you can do to make sure the map you love is still bright and sound for whoever inherits it. We'll always advise hanging it out of direct sunlight too.

Can you value a map I already own?

Yes — we provide honest valuations for insurance, sale and probate, whether you're insuring a collection, settling an estate, or simply curious what the map on your wall might be worth. We'll examine it properly: identify the mapmaker, date and edition, assess condition and any restoration, and place it in the context of what comparable examples actually sell for, not an inflated guess. If you're thinking of selling, we'll tell you frankly whether it's something we'd buy or take on commission, and what a realistic figure looks like. And if a much-loved map turns out to be worth little in money, we'll say so gently — sentimental value is real value too, and not everything needs a price to be worth keeping and framing well.

How should I look after antique maps at home?

The two great enemies are light and damp, so start there: hang maps out of direct sunlight (UV fades ink and colour fast) and away from radiators, bathrooms and outside walls that get cold and damp. Conservation framing handles most of the rest, protecting the paper from acid and pollutants and holding it flat without stress. Never dry-mount, laminate or trim an antique map, never use ordinary tape on it, and don't try to "clean" it yourself — well-meant repairs and cleaning do more damage than almost anything. If a map is stored rather than framed, keep it flat in acid-free folders somewhere cool, dry and dark. And if something does go wrong — a tear, a spill, foxing spreading — bring it to us before you try to fix it; a conservator can work wonders, but only with what hasn't already been botched.

Do you buy maps, or take them on commission?

Both, depending on the piece. We're always interested in genuine antique maps, charts and celestial prints, whether a single inherited map or a whole collection, and we'll give you an honest assessment of what we can offer or what it might achieve on commission through the shop. Bring it in or send us clear photographs of the whole map and any text, borders and the reverse, along with rough dimensions, and we'll tell you what we think it is and whether it's for us. We'd always rather be straight with you — if your map is worth more to a specialist auction or another dealer, we'll say so. A reputation for honesty is the only thing that lasts in this trade, and it's taken us since 1998 to build ours.
