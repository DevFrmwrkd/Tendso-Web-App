MOVEMENT 01 / THE WORKSHOPA CENTURY OF ENGINEERING

A good piano is a century of engineering. Treat it like one.

Hammer & String is a piano workshop in Bath, run by restorers who trained the long way and have spent two decades with the lids up. We opened in 2004 because too many good instruments were being scrapped — declared "beyond economical repair" by people who'd never opened them — when in truth they needed patience, the right parts, and someone who understood how they were built.

A piano is thousands of moving parts under tons of string tension, and every one affects the touch and the tone. We rebuild actions, replace hammers and dampers, re-string and re-pin, restore soundboards and refinish cases — and just as often we regulate and voice an instrument that only needed setting up properly. We'll always tell you honestly whether a piano is worth the work.

We look after everything from the family upright that taught three generations to concert grands on the recital platform, and we tune for homes, schools, churches and halls across the region. Whatever you play and whatever it's worth, the aim is the same: an instrument that responds under your fingers and holds its tune, so the piano gets played instead of dusted.

Is my old piano actually worth restoring?

Very often, yes — and we'll tell you honestly either way, because we'd rather keep your trust than sell you work you don't need. A well-made piano from a reputable maker, even a century old, usually has a soundboard, frame and case worth building on; what wears out is the action, strings, hammers and felts, all of which we can renew. The pianos genuinely not worth restoring tend to be cheaply built to begin with, or so far gone that a rebuild costs more than a good secondhand instrument. We assess yours specifically — its make, condition and what you want from it — rather than giving you a blanket answer, and we'll always point you to the sensible choice even when it isn't the most expensive one.

How often should a piano be tuned?

For most homes, twice a year is the sweet spot — pianos drift with the seasons as humidity changes, and two tunings a year keeps yours reliably at pitch and catches small problems early. A piano that's played hard, used for teaching, or kept somewhere with big temperature swings may want tuning more often; a concert instrument might be tuned before every performance. If a piano has been left for years it may need a couple of tunings fairly close together to bring it back to pitch and hold there. We'll recommend a schedule based on your instrument and how you use it, and remind you when it's due.

What's the difference between tuning, regulating and voicing?

They're three different things and a piano usually needs all three at some point. Tuning adjusts the string tension so the notes are at the correct pitch relative to each other — it's about being in tune. Regulating adjusts the thousands of mechanical parts of the action so the keys respond evenly and precisely — it's about how the piano feels under your fingers. Voicing shapes the hammer felt to control the tone, from mellow to bright — it's about the character of the sound. Many pianos people think are "past it" are simply badly regulated and voiced; sorting those out can transform an instrument without a full rebuild.

Do you work on the piano in my home or take it away?

It depends on the job. Tuning, regulating, voicing and most repairs we do in your home — there's no need to move the piano, and it's better tuned in the room it lives in. Full restorations and rebuilds come to the workshop, where we can strip the action right down, re-string, work on the soundboard and refinish the case properly in controlled conditions, then return it and let it settle before a final tuning. We arrange specialist piano moving for that (pianos are heavy, awkward and easily damaged by amateurs), and we'll always tell you upfront whether a job can be done at home or needs the bench.

Can you help me buy a secondhand piano?

Please ask us before you buy, not after — it's the cheapest advice you'll ever get. A piano can look immaculate and be mechanically exhausted, or look tatty and be a gem underneath; the case tells you very little about the instrument. We can inspect a piano you're considering, tell you what it really needs and what that will cost, and help you judge whether the asking price makes sense. We're not selling you anything, so the advice is genuinely impartial. Many people have avoided an expensive mistake, or spotted a bargain, by having us look first.

Do you tune for schools, churches and venues?

Yes — a good part of our work is looking after institutional instruments, from school and church uprights to hall and recital grands. We can set up a regular maintenance schedule so the piano is always ready when it's needed, handle the repairs and regulation that heavy shared use demands, and work around your timetable and events. For performance venues we tune to concert standard on the day, and we're used to the discretion and reliability that requires. Get in touch with what you've got and how it's used and we'll put together a plan that keeps it playable.
