POUR 01 / THE CHANDLERYNAMED, NOTHING HIDDEN

A candle is the only thing you buy to watch it disappear.

So it had better be worth watching. We started Wick & Tallow in 2015 because most high-street candles are a mystery — vague "fragrance", wax you can't identify, and a burn that tunnels down the middle and gutters out early. We wanted the opposite: everything named, nothing hidden, and a flame that behaves right to the base.

Every candle is poured by hand in small batches at the bench by the window. We blend our own scents by the season — bright and green in spring, resinous and warm by winter — and we cure each batch properly before it ever goes on the shelf. A rushed candle is a bad candle; the wait is the work.

We hand-dip tapers the old way too, and we take our refills seriously: bring your vessel back, we'll re-pour it for less. A candle shop that only sells you new glass every time has missed the point of a craft that's meant to last.

What wax do you use, and why does it matter?

We work in clean plant waxes and beeswax, never paraffin, and we say which is which on every label. Wax choice changes how a candle burns, how it throws scent and what it puts into your air — so hiding it behind the word "fragrance" has always seemed wrong to us. Different waxes suit different jobs: beeswax for tapers and a warm natural glow, plant blends for jars and a clean cold-and-hot scent throw. Ask and we'll talk you through it happily.

Why do your candles cost more than supermarket ones?

Because they're poured by hand in small batches, cured for weeks before sale, scented with proper loads of quality oils, and built to burn clean to the base rather than tunnel and die early. You're also buying the refill loop — bring the vessel back and the next pour is cheaper. Per hour of clean, well-scented burn, they work out very fair; per shortcut taken, there simply aren't any.

What's tunnelling and how do I avoid it?

Tunnelling is when a candle burns straight down the middle leaving a ring of wasted wax up the sides. It usually comes from a poorly matched wick or a rushed pour — problems we design out. Your part is simple: on the first burn, let the melt pool reach the full edge of the vessel before you blow it out (usually an hour or two). Do that once and a well-made candle will burn evenly for its whole life.

How do the refills work?

Bring your empty Wick & Tallow vessel back to the shop — cleaned or not, we'll sort it — and we'll re-pour it with the scent of your choice for less than the price of a new one. It keeps good glass and ceramic out of the bin and rewards you for it. We can usually do popular scents while you browse; unusual ones or big batches we'll pour and have ready for collection.

Can I book a workshop for a group?

Yes — workshops are two hours at the bench where you blend a scent and pour your own candle to take home and cure. We run open sessions for individuals and pairs, and private bookings for hen-dos, birthdays and work teams. Groups get the run of the scent bar and a lot of laughing; tell us numbers and any preferences and we'll set it up. Everyone leaves with something they actually made.

Do you supply shops, cafés or weddings?

Gladly, and it's a real part of what we do. For trade we'll pour to order in proper batches, in your vessel or ours, using a house blend or a scent we develop to your brief. For weddings we do favours, aisle and table candles, and bespoke scents for the day. Give us a realistic lead time and we'll give you candles that smell like you meant it.
