MISE 01 / THE KITCHENFOUNDED ON A HECKLE

Founded on a heckle.

The Tuesday Kitchen started when our founder — fifteen years in professional kitchens — watched a TV chef "simplify" a recipe to nineteen steps and shouted at the television. The shout became a pop-up, the pop-up became a waiting list, and the waiting list became this school.

The teaching principle is stolen from restaurant kitchens: watch it done once, do it yourself immediately, get corrected while your hands still remember. You cook at your own bench, with your own mistakes. Our job is making the mistakes useful.

Every class ends the same way: the benches get wiped, the long table gets set, and everyone eats what they made with wine and honest commentary. Half our regulars say the table is the reason they come back. We take this as culinary success.

MISE 03 / THE METHODSTOLEN FROM REAL KITCHENS

The house method.

RULE 1/3

Taught by working cooks.

Everyone who teaches here still cooks for a living. You're learning the moves that survive a hundred covers, not the ones that survive a photoshoot.

RULE 2/3

Your bench, your mistakes.

Demonstrations are short; your hands-on time isn't. You'll cook everything yourself — slightly wrong, then corrected, then right. That order matters.

RULE 3/3

The long table is the lesson.

Cooking ends in eating together, every class, no exceptions. Food you made, wine we chose, and the best conversations in Stockport.

MISE 07 / ASKED AT THE PASSAPRON STRINGS HALF-TIED

Asked at the pass.

Anything else — ring 0161 480 7752. During class we're elbow-deep in someone's béchamel; leave a message.

I can barely cook. Will I embarrass myself?

You will be in the majority. Classes are built for genuine beginners with a couple of benches' worth of stretch for improvers. The only embarrassing thing in this kitchen is pretending you know something you don't — chefs can smell it.

What's included?

Everything: ingredients, aprons, knives, recipe cards, the meal at the end and the wine with it. You bring yourself and a container for leftovers. Seriously, bring the container — people forget and mourn.

Can you handle dietary requirements?

Yes — tell us when you book. Vegetarian and vegan benches run in every class; allergies get a dedicated prep zone and an instructor briefing. Coeliac bakers: the bread day has a dedicated gluten-free bench, run properly.

Are the classes suitable for kids?

The regular classes are 16+, mostly because of wine and knife pace. School-holiday family sessions run twice a year — one adult, one child, two aprons. They book out in about a week.

How do gift vouchers work?

A voucher covers any class of that value, valid twelve months, extended without drama if life happens. It's the most-given gift in the building and arrives looking like something, not a PDF apology.

Can I book the whole kitchen?

Twelve benches, your people, your menu — teams, birthdays, hen dos, one extremely competitive divorce party. Evenings and Sundays. We plan the menu with you and referee the ravioli contest.
