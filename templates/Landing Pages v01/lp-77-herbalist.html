FILE 01 / THE APOTHECARYCAREFUL, NOT CREDULOUS

A herbalist who will tell you to see your GP.

Fennel & Fox is run by a herbalist trained to degree level and registered with a professional body — which means a proper case history, an understanding of the medicines you're already on, and a red-flag list that ends the conversation with "this needs a doctor, not a tincture". Herbalism done well is careful, not credulous.

The dispensary is the heart of it: a wall of labelled tinctures, dried herbs in Kilner jars, and a bench where blends are made up for one person at a time. Nothing here is off a pallet with a lifestyle brand on it. It's weighed, blended, labelled and dated in front of you.

We work alongside conventional medicine, not against it. Plenty of what we do is unglamorous — sleep, digestion, skin, the long tail of feeling-not-quite-right — and plenty of appointments end with us sending you elsewhere. An apothecary you can trust is one that knows its limits.

Is a medical herbalist actually qualified?

A registered medical herbalist trains to degree level in the same medical sciences a nurse or doctor covers — anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology — alongside the clinical use of plants, and is registered with a professional body that sets a code of practice and requires insurance and ongoing training. It is not a weekend course. We're happy to show you our registration.

Will herbs interfere with my medication?

They can, which is exactly why we take a full medication history before dispensing anything and rule out anything that interacts. This is the single most important thing a proper herbalist does and the single thing an off-the-shelf supplement shop can't. Always tell us — and your doctor and pharmacist — everything you're taking.

Can you treat serious conditions?

We work alongside conventional medicine for many long-term and everyday complaints, but we do not treat serious disease in place of your doctor, and we'll tell you plainly when something needs medical attention. If you have red-flag symptoms we'll send you to your GP or A&E first. Herbalism is a complement to medical care, not a replacement for it.

What happens in a consultation?

The first appointment is about an hour: a detailed history of your health, symptoms, sleep, digestion, stress and medications, then a discussion of what's realistic and what might help. If a herbal approach is sensible, we blend and explain it; if it isn't, we say so. Follow-ups are shorter and focus on how things are going.

Are your remedies safe in pregnancy or for children?

Some herbs are fine, many are not, and the dose and context matter enormously — so we're especially careful here and will often decline or refer. Always tell us if you're pregnant, breastfeeding or asking about a child, and never assume "natural" means "safe in any amount". We'd rather turn work away than take a risk.

Do I need an appointment, or can I just buy tea?

You're very welcome to come in for loose teas, dried herbs and over-the-counter balms without an appointment — the shop side is open to browse. Bespoke tinctures and anything for a specific health concern need a consultation first, so we can do it properly and safely. Pop in, and we'll point you to the right one.
