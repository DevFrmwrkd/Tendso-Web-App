LIGHT 01 / THE STUDIOBENCHES BY THE WINDOW

Glass is the only material that needs the sun to finish it.

Everything else you can judge under a workshop light. Stained glass you can't — it isn't finished until it's in the wall with the morning behind it. That's the whole discipline: designing for a light you won't see until the day it's installed, and getting it right anyway.

The studio does two things that rarely share a roof: new commissions and heritage conservation. The new work keeps the craft alive; the conservation keeps us honest, because there's no better teacher than taking apart a window a medieval glazier made and finding out how they solved a problem you're still struggling with.

Cutting, leading, cementing, glass-painting and kiln-firing all happen at the benches by the window. We're accredited for church and listed-building work, which means when the diocese calls, we already speak the language.

LIGHT 04 / HOW A COMMISSION GOESLOOK → CARTOON → FIRE → GLAZE
  1. STEP 01

    Talk & look at the light

    We visit, measure the opening, and note the aspect — which way it faces decides half the design. Bring ideas; we'll bring glass samples.

  2. STEP 02

    Cartoon & sample

    A full-size cartoon and a fired glass sample so you can see real colour in real light before committing. Approve it in the flesh, not on a screen.

  3. STEP 03

    Cut, lead, paint, fire

    Made at the bench — glass cut to the cartoon, leaded up, painted and kiln-fired, then cemented and left to cure. The slow part, done properly.

  4. STEP 04

    Glazed & sealed

    Fitted into the opening, weatherproofed, and — where needed — protected with isothermal secondary glazing. Then we wait for a sunny day with you.

How much does a stained glass window cost?

Genuinely, it depends — on size, complexity, how much glass painting it needs, and whether it's new work or conservation. A small front-door panel and a painted church memorial window are different worlds. We quote after seeing the opening; the survey is free and the quote is honest.

What's the difference between stained glass and leaded glass?

Leaded glass is coloured glass cut and joined with lead came. Stained glass adds hand-painted detail — faces, drapery, shading — fired into the surface in a kiln. Most windows are a mix. We do both, which is rarer than you'd think.

Can old windows really be saved?

More often than people fear. Bowing, cracked quarries and failing lead are usually repairable, sometimes in situ. We survey first and tell you plainly: conserve, relead, or (rarely) replace. We don't condemn a window to sell you a new one.

Do you do church and listed-building work?

Yes — it's half of what we do, to accredited conservation standards, with the faculty and DAC paperwork experience that heritage work demands. We're used to working with dioceses, architects and conservation officers.

How long does a commission take?

A domestic panel, six to twelve weeks. A painted church window or a conservation project, several months — the kiln, the curing and the paperwork all take the time they take. We give you a real programme and keep you posted with photos.

Will a new window suit an old house?

It can, beautifully, if it's designed for the building rather than dropped into it. We'll steer the design to sit right with your architecture and your light. Sometimes the most sympathetic thing is boldly contemporary; sometimes it's quiet. We'll be honest about which.
