TIDE 01 / THE BOATSFIFTY METRES, ONE BARROW

Fifty metres from boat to ice.

The Slab is the shortest supply chain we could physically build: the Marie-H and the Two Brothers tie up at the harbour wall, and their catch travels fifty metres in a barrow to our counter. What they land is what we sell. When the weather keeps the boats in, the slab is thinner and we say so.

That honesty costs us sales on the bad-weather days and earns them back tenfold on the rest. Regulars know that if we're recommending gurnard over the cod, there's a reason, and the reason is this morning.

We fillet, pin-bone, skin and butterfly at the counter — partly because it's better done fresh, mostly because watching it done is how people stop being scared of cooking fish.

TIDE 03 / THE RULESWRITTEN IN CHALK, KEPT IN STONE

House rules.

Day-boats only.

No trawler catch that's been at sea a week. Two named boats, out and back on one tide. Shorter trips mean better fish and better grounds.

The board tells the truth.

Boat, ground, method, chalked daily. If the weather kept the fleet in, the board says so and the slab is small. We'd rather sell less than lie.

We'll talk you out of it.

If the sole is tired and the gurnard is perfect, you're going home with gurnard. The recommendation is the product.

TIDE 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERUSUALLY WHILE WE'RE FILLETING

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 01227 772 411. Mornings are loud; keep trying, or message us.

What time should I come?

The slab is fullest from 7am. By mid-afternoon the good stuff is going; on summer Saturdays it's gone by two. If you want something specific, ring in the morning and we'll set it aside until four.

Will you fillet it for me?

Yes — filleting, pin-boning, skinning, portioning, all included, done while you wait. Take the bones; fish stock is twenty minutes and makes you feel like a different class of person.

Why don't you have salmon and tuna every day?

Because our boats don't catch them. The smoked salmon comes from a Scottish smokehouse we trust, but the fresh slab is what the Marie-H and the Two Brothers land. That's the deal that makes everything else true.

Is the fish frozen?

Nothing on the slab has been frozen. If you're eating it raw — and some days the slab absolutely justifies it — we'll blast-freeze it for you first, which is the food-safe way to do it at home too.

How do I know it's fresh?

Look at it: bright eyes, red gills, shine, no smell but the sea. Then look at the board — every fish carries its boat and its ground. Then ask us; we love this question.

What if the weather's been bad?

The slab is small and we say so. We'd rather send you home with two perfect herring than a week-old plaice. Follow the board's mood; it never lies.

Do you take orders for Christmas?

Yes — the book opens in November and fills fast. Whole turbot, oysters by the hundred, and the annual argument about brining. Ring early.
