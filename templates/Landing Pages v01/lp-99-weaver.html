PICK 01 / THE STUDIOA YARD THAT LASTS A CENTURY

A machine can weave a mile of cloth in an hour. We'd rather weave a yard that lasts a century.

Warp & Weft is a handweaving studio in the old mill town of Hebden Bridge, where cloth has been made for centuries. We weave on floor looms the traditional way — dressing the loom with the warp thread by painstaking thread, then throwing the weft one pick at a time — because handwoven cloth has a life, a drape and a durability that no machine-made length can touch. It is slow, meditative, occasionally maddening work, and we wouldn't swap it for anything.

We founded the studio in 2008 in a former weaving shed, working only in natural fibres — wool, linen, cotton, silk — much of it dyed by hand, and we make rugs, throws, blankets, scarves and lengths of cloth, each one woven start to finish by a single pair of hands. Because it's handmade, we can weave to your commission: your colours, your fibres, your dimensions, a rug for a particular room or a cloth for a particular purpose, with the honest small irregularities that tell you a human made it.

Alongside the weaving we repair and re-weave treasured textiles — a worn patch in an heirloom rug, a moth-eaten blanket, a length of cloth that deserves saving rather than discarding — and we teach. Our classes run from a first taste of the loom for absolute beginners through to advanced technique for keen weavers, in small groups in the studio. Whether you want to own something handwoven, save something you love, or learn to weave it yourself, this is a place where thread becomes something lasting.

PICK 03 / THE DIFFERENCEWHY HANDWOVEN

Why handwoven.

01 / 03

Woven a pick at a time.

Every rug, throw and cloth is woven by hand on a floor loom, one pass of the shuttle at a time, by a single weaver from first thread to finished piece. Slow, yes — and built to last a lifetime.

02 / 03

Natural fibres, hand-dyed.

We work only in wool, linen, cotton and silk, much of it dyed by hand, because natural fibre drapes, wears and ages in a way synthetics never will. Real materials make real cloth.

03 / 03

We save what you love.

A worn heirloom rug or a moth-holed blanket isn't rubbish; it's a repair. We re-weave and mend treasured textiles so the things you've lived with can be lived with for years more.

Before you commission.

Why is a handwoven rug or throw more expensive than a shop-bought one?

Because you are buying many days of skilled human work and genuine natural materials, not a machine-made length run off by the mile — and the difference is in every thread. A handweaver dresses the loom by threading the warp one end at a time (a job that can itself take a day or more), then weaves the whole piece by throwing the shuttle pick by pick, beating each row home by hand, before finishing it off the loom. A rug that takes a machine minutes takes us days or weeks. On top of that we work only in natural fibres — wool, linen, cotton, silk — often hand-dyed, which cost far more than the synthetics in cheap textiles but drape, wear, breathe and age incomparably better. What you get for the price is a piece with real substance and character, made to be used hard and last for generations, with the small honest irregularities that mark the human hand. It is the difference between something you replace and something you hand down.

Can I commission a rug or cloth in my own colours and size?

Yes — bespoke commissions are the heart of the studio and one of the real joys of buying handwoven rather than off a shelf. Because we weave each piece by hand from scratch, we can make it to your colours, your choice of fibres, your weave structure and your exact dimensions, whether that's a rug sized precisely to a particular room or hallway, a throw to sit with a specific scheme, or a length of cloth for a designer or maker. We'll talk through what the piece is for and where it'll live, help you choose fibres and colours (hand-dyeing yarn where you need a particular shade we can't buy), and weave a small sample so you can see and feel the actual cloth — colour, weight, texture and drape — before we commit the full piece to the loom. From there it's woven by hand, which takes days to weeks depending on size and complexity. The result is genuinely yours: made to your brief, by hand, and unrepeatable. Tell us what you have in mind and we'll design it with you.

Can you repair or re-weave a damaged or moth-eaten textile?

Very often, yes, and it's some of the most satisfying work we do — saving a loved textile is always better than discarding it. We repair and re-weave worn patches, frayed edges, moth damage and holes in handwoven and quality textiles: rugs, blankets, throws and heirloom pieces. Depending on the damage and the piece, that might mean carefully darning or patching in matching yarn, or actually re-weaving a damaged section on the loom to blend with the original — and where we can, we match the old fibre and colour closely enough that the repair is hard to find. We'll always assess honestly first: some textiles are beyond economic repair, or are better conserved as they are than heavily reworked, and we'll tell you so rather than take on work that isn't worth it. But a great many pieces that people assume are ruined can be brought back into use. Bring it in or send us photographs of the whole piece and the damage, and we'll tell you honestly what's possible.

I've never woven before — are the classes really for beginners?

Absolutely — genuine beginners are exactly who our first-taster and introductory classes are for, and no experience whatsoever is needed. Weaving looks intimidating from the outside, all those threads and moving parts, but the basics are wonderfully approachable with someone beside you, and most people are weaving actual cloth within their first session and leaving with something they made themselves. We keep classes small so you get real hands-on attention, and we start at the very beginning: how a loom works, dressing it with the warp, throwing the shuttle, beating and building the weft, and finishing your cloth. From that first taste you can go as far as you like — we run progressively more advanced classes on colour, pattern, structure and finer technique for those who catch the bug (and many do). It's absorbing, meditative and deeply satisfying, a proper antidote to screens, and there's a warm community of weavers here to fall in with. Come and throw a shuttle; we think you'll be hooked.

What can I do if I want to keep weaving but don't own a loom?

Hire studio time on our looms — it's a service we offer precisely because a good floor loom is a big and expensive thing to own and house, and we don't want that to stop keen weavers from weaving. Once you've learned the basics with us (through a class, or if you're already experienced), you can book sessions to work on our floor looms in the studio, with guidance and help on hand as much or as little as you need, whether you're working through a project, practising a new technique, or simply weaving for the pleasure and calm of it. It's a lovely way to keep the craft going between projects, to weave something larger than a home setup would allow, or to be sure you truly love it before investing in a loom of your own. Studio-time weavers become part of the community here, and much learning and friendship happens over adjacent looms. Ask us about session availability and rates; we'll help you find a rhythm that fits.

Are your pieces hard-wearing, and how do I care for handwoven textiles?

Yes — handwoven pieces in natural fibre are typically far more hard-wearing than people expect, which is part of why we make them; a well-woven wool rug is built to be walked on daily for decades, and a good throw or blanket only gets better with age and use. Care is mostly common sense and gentleness. Rugs benefit from regular gentle vacuuming (without a fierce beater bar), rotating occasionally so they wear evenly, and prompt, careful attention to spills rather than harsh scrubbing. Throws, blankets and scarves are best hand-washed or dry-cleaned as the fibre dictates — we give specific care notes with every piece and are always happy to advise. Wool naturally resists dirt and is a little self-cleaning, but does need protecting from moth when stored: keep pieces clean, aired and, for long storage, protected. And crucially — when something does eventually wear or suffer damage, don't discard it; bring it back to us. A handwoven piece is made to be repaired and to last, and we'll help you keep yours in use for a very long time indeed.
