SLOT 01 / THE COUNTEREIGHTEEN STOOLS, ONE POT

A counter, a pot, a stopwatch.

Menya Kasa is eighteen stools around an open kitchen the size of a generous wardrobe. The tonkotsu pot goes on at four in the morning and doesn't stop rolling for eighteen hours; the noodle machine — imported, temperamental, named Noriko — runs at two every afternoon and the day's noodles rest exactly four hours before service.

The founder trained for three years at a twelve-seat shop in Fukuoka where the master spoke five words of English, four of which were corrections. The discipline came home intact: a short menu, obsessive stock work, and the belief that a ramen bar should do one thing at a level that makes queuing rational.

There are no bookings, no delivery apps, and no ramen after the pot is empty. We know. That's the deal that makes the bowl worth it.

SLOT 03 / HOUSE RULESNON-NEGOTIABLE, ALL THREE

House rules.

一

The pot dictates everything.

Eighteen hours of broth cannot be rushed, stretched or faked. When it's gone, we close — selling a thin bowl at 9pm would be a lie with noodles in it.

二

Noodles made at two.

Cut daily, rested four hours, matched to each broth's viscosity. Noriko the noodle machine has one job and a better work ethic than most of us.

三

Four bowls, zero bloat.

No katsu curry, no bao, no "Asian fusion" page two. Four bowls we can defend individually, cooked a thousand times each. That's the whole restaurant.

SLOT 07 / ASKED IN THE QUEUEANSWERED OVER THE STEAM

Asked in the queue.

Anything else — ring 0115 947 8812 before four. After four, the pot outranks the phone.

Can I book a table?

No — eighteen stools, one queue, first come first slurped. The queue moves faster than it looks: most of the time you're seated within twenty minutes. Come at five or after eight (pot permitting) for the shortest waits.

Why do you close when the broth runs out?

Because the alternative is watering it down, and you'd taste it immediately. The pot is eighteen hours of work; it makes a fixed number of honest bowls. When they're gone, the only honest thing left to serve is an apology.

Is the vegan bowl actually good or just there?

It outsold the shoyu last month. Roasted field mushrooms make a broth with real depth, and the smoked tofu earns its place. It's a bowl we're proud of, not a box we ticked.

Can I get it delivered?

Never. Noodles keep degrading from the second they hit broth — a delivered ramen is a photograph of a meal. Twenty minutes in a box would undo eighteen hours in the pot.

What's kae-dama and how do I do it?

A second helping of fresh noodles for your remaining broth, a Fukuoka tradition. Flag it while you still have a third of your broth left — timing matters and we take it seriously.

Kids? Groups? Allergies?

Kids welcome on laps or stools they can reach the counter from. Groups: sixes split across the counter, that's physics. Allergens: full matrix on the wall and the tare recipes don't change, so the answers stay true.
