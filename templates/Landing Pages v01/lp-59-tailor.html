PATTERN 01 / THE WORKROOMPAPER FIRST, CLOTH SECOND

Paper first. Cloth second.

Every Thread & Chalk garment begins as a paper pattern, drafted by hand from your measurements and your posture — the dropped shoulder, the longer left arm, the way you actually stand rather than the way a mannequin does. That pattern is yours forever. It hangs in the pattern room with your name on the corner.

There are two cutters and one machinist, and the person who takes your measurements is the person who drafts your pattern. Nothing is outsourced to a factory in the small print. The canvas is floating and sewn, not glued; a glued chest is how a jacket dies in five years and we won't be party to it.

We make suits, jackets, overcoats and the occasional wildly specific waistcoat. We will talk you out of a fashionable lapel and into one you'll still respect in a decade. That conversation is included.

PATTERN 03 / STANDARDSNON-NEGOTIABLE, ALL THREE

What makes it bespoke.

STD 1/3

Your pattern, kept for life.

Drafted by hand, filed under your name, reused for every future garment. Bespoke means the pattern exists; if it doesn't, it isn't bespoke, whatever the label says.

STD 2/3

Floating canvas, always.

The chest is stitched, not fused. It moulds to you, breathes, and survives the dry cleaner. A glued jacket bubbles and dies; ours are built to be re-lined, not replaced.

STD 3/3

The measurer is the cutter.

One pair of hands from tape to shears. Nothing lost in translation to a factory, because there is no factory — just the bench you're standing at.

PATTERN 04 / THE PROCESSMEASURE → BASTE → FORWARD → FINISH
  1. Sitting 01

    Measure & draft

    An hour of measuring, posture and cloth. We draft your paper pattern from it — the document the whole suit descends from.

  2. Sitting 02

    The baste fitting

    You try the suit half-made, tacked in white thread. We rip and re-cut at this stage; that's what it's for. Bring honesty about how it feels.

  3. Sitting 03

    The forward fitting

    Closer now, real stitching, fine adjustments to balance and length. Usually the last time we take a shear to it.

  4. Sitting 04

    Finish & collect

    Hand-finished buttonholes, your choice of lining, pressed and delivered. Your pattern goes on the rack for next time.

What's the difference between bespoke and made-to-measure?

Made-to-measure adjusts a block pattern to your dimensions. Bespoke drafts a pattern from scratch, for you, then cuts and fits it over several sittings. Bespoke costs more and fits better, and the gap widens every year the garment is worn. If a shop can't show you a paper pattern with your name on it, it's made-to-measure.

How long does a suit take?

Twelve to sixteen weeks for a first bespoke suit, across three fittings. Your second suit is faster because the pattern already exists. Weddings we plan backwards from the date and have never been late — ask us how tight is too tight before you panic.

What does it cost?

Cloth and complexity set the price, so it's a conversation, not a sticker. What we'll promise: the quote is fixed once the cloth is chosen, and nothing is added later. Bring a budget and we'll build the best suit inside it, honestly.

Can I bring my own cloth?

Yes, though we'll assess it first — some cloth is lovely and unsuitable, and it's kinder to say so before we cut. Our bunch books cover mills we trust, but a length you've carried home from a trip has a story we're glad to build into a jacket.

Do you make for women?

Yes. Bespoke tailoring for women is some of our favourite work and there's far too little of it done well. Same process, same floating canvas, same pattern kept for life.

What if I lose or gain weight?

Small changes we alter — that's what the inlays are for; we leave cloth in the seams precisely so a suit can follow you a size in either direction. Big changes mean a new pattern, but the old one stays on file in case you return to it. Bodies move; good tailoring expects it.
