ALT 01 / THE FLIGHTTHE OLDEST WAY TO FLY

Ballooning is the oldest way to fly, and still the only one that feels like magic.

Level & Drift fly hot air balloons over the Somerset Levels, and we've been doing it since 2003 because there is genuinely nothing else like it. You lift off with a roar of the burner and then — silence. No engine, no wings, just the wind carrying you and the whole flat sweep of the moors, the rhynes and droves, Glastonbury Tor rising out of the morning mist, unrolling beneath your feet. People come down changed by it.

Every flight is commanded by a CAA-licensed commercial balloon pilot with thousands of hours over this particular sky, because ballooning is entirely about reading weather and wind, and local knowledge is everything. We fly at the two times the air is calm and the light is glorious — just after sunrise and in the last hours before sunset — and we only fly when conditions are genuinely right, which is the single most important thing we do. A cancelled flight is a disappointment; a rushed one is a risk, and we don't take it.

We fly shared-basket flights where you'll share the sky with a handful of others, private charters for just your group, and the big occasions — proposals (we've never had a no), landmark birthdays, anniversaries, ashes-scattering flights of great tenderness. Every flight ends the traditional way: gently down into a Somerset field, a Champagne toast to the aeronauts' oath, and a flight certificate. The retrieve crew finds you; you just enjoy the coming-down.

ALT 07 / BEFORE YOU FLYWEATHER, SAFETY & THE MORNING

Before you fly.

Anything else — ring 01458 555 210. We love talking about the sky, and we'll always be straight about the weather.

Why can't you just tell me the date I'll fly?

Because hot air balloons fly on the weather's terms, not ours, and any operator who promises you a guaranteed fixed date is either going to disappoint you or take a risk we never will. Balloons need light, stable winds (under about 8–10 knots), good visibility, no rain and no threatening weather, and those conditions typically only occur reliably around sunrise and in the last hours before sunset. So when you book, you book onto a flight slot — a morning or an evening — and we confirm whether it's flying the evening before, once we have a firm forecast. If it's not right, we simply rebook you onto the next available slot, as many times as it takes, at no extra cost. It can feel frustrating if you're keen, but it is the single biggest reason we've flown for over twenty years without incident. A flight worth having is worth waiting for the right morning.

Is it safe, and is it scary?

Ballooning is one of aviation's safest and oldest forms of flight, and the honest experience of it is far calmer than people expect. Every flight is commanded by a CAA-licensed commercial balloon pilot, the balloon is maintained and inspected to strict aviation standards, and — crucially — we only ever fly in genuinely suitable conditions. As for scary: because the basket moves with the wind rather than against it, there's no wind in your face and very little sense of speed, and because you're standing in a solid basket rather than looking through a window, most people who consider themselves nervous of heights are completely fine. There's the roar of the burner, then long stretches of extraordinary silence and stillness. Far and away the most common thing we hear on landing is "that was so much more peaceful than I imagined."

What should I wear and bring, and how fit do I need to be?

Dress as if for a country walk on the morning of the flight — layers, long trousers, and flat, sturdy shoes or boots you don't mind getting damp in a dewy or muddy field (no sandals or heels). A hat is sensible as the burner is warm overhead, and bring a camera or phone with a strap. You don't need to be especially fit, but you do need to be able to climb into and out of the basket unaided (it's about waist height, so a big step up and over) and to stand for the duration of the flight, which is around an hour — there's nowhere to sit. For that reason there's a lower age guideline (children need to see over the basket edge) and we'll talk through any mobility concerns honestly when you book. If in doubt, just ask; we'd always rather have the conversation than have someone struggle.

How long is the flight, and how long is the whole morning?

The flight itself is typically around an hour in the air, though it varies with the wind and where it takes us — we can't steer precisely, only choose our height to catch different wind directions, which is part of the adventure. The whole experience, however, takes the best part of a morning or evening, and you should set aside around four hours in total: meeting at the launch site, watching (and often helping with) the inflation, the flight, the landing wherever the wind delivers us, the Champagne toast, and the drive back from the landing field to the start in our retrieve vehicle. It's a leisurely, unrushed affair and that's the joy of it — this is not a fairground ride but a proper expedition into the sky. Bring the morning; don't schedule anything tight afterwards.

Can I book a private flight or a proposal, and can people watch?

Yes to all three, and they're some of our favourite flights to run. A private charter gives you the whole basket for your own group rather than sharing with strangers, ideal for families, couples, celebrations or teams; it costs more per person than a shared flight but buys you the sky to yourselves. Proposals are a speciality and we're delighted to help — we'll safely carry and hide the ring, help you plan the moment and the timing, brief the pilot to give you privacy at the right instant, and (whisper it) our success rate is one hundred percent. Friends and family are very welcome to come and watch the launch, which is a spectacle in itself, and if they'd like to follow the retrieve crew to guess where you'll land, they can be there for the toast too. Just tell us the plan when you book and we'll build the morning around it.

What happens if the weather cancels my flight, or I need to rearrange?

If we scrub a flight for weather — which we will, without hesitation, whenever conditions aren't right — we simply rebook you onto the next suitable slot at no cost, and we'll keep doing that until you fly; your booking and any voucher never expires because of weather cancellations. If you need to rearrange from your side, just give us reasonable notice and we'll move you to another available date within your voucher's validity. Gift vouchers are open-dated and valid for two full flying seasons precisely so there's never pressure to fly before the sky's ready. The only thing we ask is patience and a little flexibility around dawn and dusk; give us those, and we'll give you a morning you remember for the rest of your life. Nobody has ever regretted waiting for the right one.
