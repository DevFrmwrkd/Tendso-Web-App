GORE 01 / THE STUDIOTHE FOUR-CENTURY METHOD

A globe is a flat map that has made its peace with a round earth.

Meridian Works is a small globe-making studio in Bath, and we make globes the way they've been made for four centuries — because no shortcut has ever bettered it. It begins with the map, printed in tapering segments called gores, and the true craft is laying those gores, damp and stretching, onto a perfect sphere so that every coastline meets its neighbour at the seam and the poles close without a pucker. It is slow, exacting, faintly maddening work, and there is nothing else we would rather do.

We founded the studio in 1997 out of a shared obsession — one of us a cartographer, one a bookbinder — and a conviction that a globe is the most beautiful object a home or a study can hold: a scientific instrument, a work of art, and a quiet invitation to wonder, all turning on one brass axis. Every globe we make is built on a true sphere, mounted by hand, seamed at the meridian, and balanced to turn smoothly and sit level. We make them plain and scholarly or richly coloured, floor-standing or desk-sized, contemporary or in the antique manner.

Alongside the globes we draw and hand-finish bespoke maps — of an estate, a coastline, a journey, a city someone loves — and we restore antique globes that have split, faded or seized, returning them to spin again. Whether you're commissioning a globe to mark a milestone, a map of somewhere that matters, or bringing us a family heirloom that's lost its turn, you're commissioning something made slowly, by hand, to outlast everyone who'll ever touch it.

How is a real globe actually made, and why does it take so long?

A traditional terrestrial globe is made by a method that has barely changed in four hundred years, because nothing has ever improved on it — and understanding it explains the time and the cost. First the world map is drawn and printed not as a flat sheet but as a series of tapering segments called "gores", shaped so that they will curve to fit a sphere. Each gore is then dampened and laid by hand onto a perfectly true ball, stretched and smoothed so that its edges meet the next gore exactly along the seam, with the coastlines, borders and lines of latitude flowing unbroken around the globe and closing cleanly at the poles. That laying-up is the true craft: fiddly, unforgiving, and impossible to rush, because a millimetre's drift at the equator becomes a visible fault at the pole. After mounting comes colouring, sealing, and the balancing of the sphere on its axis and mount so it turns smoothly and sits level. All of it is done by hand, one globe at a time, over weeks — which is precisely why a real globe is an heirloom and a printed plastic ball is a toy.

How much does a commissioned globe cost?

A handmade globe is a significant commission and priced accordingly, because you are buying many weeks of skilled handwork, not a manufactured object — but the range is wide and we will always be honest about what your budget can achieve. A smaller desk globe, plainly finished, sits at the more accessible end; a large floor-standing globe, richly hand-coloured, mounted in a fine stand and personalised with inscriptions or a plotted journey, is a major piece and priced as one. The variables are size, the complexity of the cartography and colouring, the mount and stand, and any bespoke personalisation. Bespoke maps and restorations are quoted individually according to their scope. What every price includes is the same thing: genuine, traditional, one-at-a-time handwork by a cartographer and a maker, and an object built to outlast generations. If you tell us your budget and what the piece is for, we will tell you frankly and without pressure what we can make for it — there is usually a way to create something beautiful at a range of levels.

Can you restore my old or damaged globe?

Very probably, yes — restoring antique and vintage globes is one of the most rewarding parts of what we do, and globes that look beyond hope often aren't. The commonest problems are entirely repairable: gores that have split, lifted or gone brittle along the seams; colour that has faded, foxed or discoloured under old varnish; a mount or stand that has loosened, warped or broken; and an axis that has seized so the globe no longer turns. We assess each globe individually and with great care, because a restoration should respect the object's age and character rather than erase it — sometimes the right course is a light, sympathetic conservation rather than a full refinish, and we'll always advise honestly, including telling you when a globe is better left as a characterful original than over-restored. We can also value globes for insurance or sale. Bring it in or send us clear photographs of the whole globe, the damage, and any maker's name or date, and we'll tell you what's possible and what it would involve.

Are your globes and maps geographically accurate?

Yes — accuracy matters to us enormously, because a globe that is beautiful but wrong is a decoration, not an instrument, and we are cartographers first. Our globes and maps are drawn from proper, current geographic data and carefully checked, so coastlines, borders, place-names and the graticule of latitude and longitude are correct and true to the projection. That said, we make globes in different styles for different purposes, and we're always clear about which you're getting: a contemporary globe shows the modern world accurately; a globe made "in the antique manner" faithfully reproduces the look, and sometimes the historical geography and old place-names, of a globe of an earlier century — which is part of its charm but is a deliberate stylistic choice, not an error. If you need absolute contemporary accuracy (for an educational or presentation piece, say) we'll make that; if you want the romance of an old-world globe, we'll make that instead, and we'll make sure you know exactly which world you're commissioning.

Do you make celestial globes and bespoke maps too, not just terrestrial globes?

We do, and they're some of our favourite commissions. Celestial globes map the night sky rather than the earth's surface — the constellations, the stars and the celestial coordinate grid mounted onto a sphere by exactly the same painstaking gore method — and they make a wonderful counterpart to a terrestrial globe or a beautiful piece in their own right for astronomers and dreamers. Bespoke maps are a whole strand of the studio: hand-drawn and hand-finished maps of a particular place — a family estate, a stretch of coast, a city, the route of a great journey or expedition — designed with you and rendered as a framed piece to keep, name and treasure. Both begin the same way as a globe: a conversation about what it's for and what it should feel like, followed by design, proofing and then slow, careful making by hand. Whether it's the heavens on a sphere or a beloved corner of the earth on paper, tell us what you have in mind and we'll draw you a world.

How long does a commission take, and can you work to a deadline?

A handmade globe or bespoke map typically takes several weeks to a few months from commission to delivery, depending on its size, complexity and where we are in our order book — and because everything is made by hand, one at a time, we can't honestly compress that without compromising the work, which we won't do. For that reason we always encourage people to commission well ahead of a milestone like a significant birthday, retirement, anniversary or Christmas, and the earlier you come to us the more relaxed and considered the design stage can be. That said, we understand that the best occasions sometimes announce themselves late, and where a deadline is genuinely tight we'll always tell you honestly at the outset whether it's achievable and, if it is, exactly what's possible within the time — rather than promising and disappointing. If a full globe can't be made in time, we can sometimes arrange a beautifully presented commission certificate to give on the day, with the finished piece to follow. Talk to us early, tell us the date, and we'll be straight with you about what we can make and when.
