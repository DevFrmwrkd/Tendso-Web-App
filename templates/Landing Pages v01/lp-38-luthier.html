BENCH 01 / THE WORKSHOPONE LUTHIER, ON PURPOSE

A one-bench workshop, on purpose.

Fret & Grain is deliberately small: one luthier, one bench, one instrument apart at a time. Guitars come in with stories attached — the ferry it survived, the parent it belonged to — and you don't delegate stories to a queue of juniors.

I trained in violin repair, which sounds irrelevant until you've seen what violin people consider an acceptable glue joint. That standard came with me. Hide glue where hide glue belongs, cleats you'll never see, finish touch-ups that don't announce themselves.

The build side runs two or three instruments a year — OM and parlour bodies mostly, from timber bought in person, quarter-sawn, and stacked in the loft to think about life for a few years before it becomes anything.

Action, relief, nut slots, intonation, electronics check. The single best money-per-decibel improvement a guitar can get.

Full refrets, partial refrets, level-crown-polish. Stainless if you insist, nickel if you ask my opinion.

JOB № 02

Structural repair

Cracks, braces, bridges lifting, headstocks that met a doorframe. Hide glue, proper cleats, patience. Done once, done right.

JOB № 03

Acoustic & electric surgery

Nut and saddle work, pickup installs, wiring rescues, hardware that finally stays in tune. Diagnosis before prescription, always.

JOB № 04

Instruments where the wrong repair destroys the value — monetary or the other kind. Conservative work, documented, reversible where possible.

Two or three a year. OM and parlour patterns, your specifications argued about honestly, twelve to eighteen months. The list is the list.

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 028 9024 6631. If I'm gluing, I'll ring back when the clamps are on.

How long is the wait?

Setups usually inside two weeks. Structural work depends on what's already open on the bench — I'll give you a real date at assessment, not an optimistic one. Builds: twelve to eighteen months, and the list is honest.

Is my cheap guitar worth setting up?

Almost always yes. A £300 guitar with a proper setup beats a £900 guitar without one, and it isn't close. It's the repair I most enjoy — maximum transformation, minimum drama.

My guitar has sentimental value and I'm nervous.

That's most of my work, honestly. Conservative repairs, reversible where possible, photographed at every stage, and nothing happens to it that we haven't discussed first. It's allowed to matter.

Do you work on basses, mandolins, banjos?

Basses and mandolins, yes. Banjos, reluctantly but competently. Violins I'll assess and then refer to the violin shop that trained me — they're better at violins than I am, which is the point of referrals.

Can you match a factory finish?

For touch-ups, close enough that you'll stop being able to find it. Full refinishing I mostly advise against on anything vintage — the finish is part of the value. I'll explain the trade-offs before anything is sprayed.

What does a commission cost?

It depends on timber and spec, so quotes happen after a conversation, not before. What I can promise: the quote is fixed once agreed, and you'll see photos at every stage from plank to strings.
