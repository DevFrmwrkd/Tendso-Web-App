01 / THE SALONBECAUSE OF A LURCHER CALLED BRAM

Built for the dogs who hate the groomer's.

The Wash House exists because of a rescue lurcher called Bram who shook through every grooming appointment of his life — until his owner (now our founder) retrained as a groomer to figure out why. The answer was obvious once seen: noise, cages, strange dogs, and being rushed.

So this salon removed all four. One dog in the building at a time. No cages — dogs are towel-and-stand dried, or air-dried on the sofa if that's their preference (it's often their preference).

Half our clients arrive labelled "difficult" by other salons. Almost none of them leave that way.

03 / HOUSE RULESTHREE, NON-NEGOTIABLE

The house rules.

RULE 1/3

One dog at a time.

No barking chorus, no strange dogs, no waiting in a crate while others are done. The single-dog policy is the whole business model.

RULE 2/3

Never cage dried.

Cage dryers are efficient and horrible. We towel, stand-dry, or wait. Your dog's blood pressure is worth the extra twenty minutes.

RULE 3/3

Fear-free trained.

Both groomers certified in low-stress handling. We stop before a dog needs us to stop, which is how they learn we will.

My dog is genuinely difficult. Bitey, even.

Tell us everything when you book — the honest version. We take muzzle-trained dogs, we work slow, and we stop sessions early rather than push a dog past coping. Some dogs take three visits to finish one groom the first time. That's fine. That's the job.

Why is there only one dog at a time?

Because most grooming stress isn't the bath or the clippers — it's the environment. Strange dogs, barking, cage dryers, waiting. Remove those and "difficult" dogs frequently just... aren't.

How long does a groom take?

Ninety minutes for a full groom, an hour for a bath and tidy. Nervous dogs get longer slots at no extra charge, because rushing them undoes months of progress.

Can I stay and watch?

For puppy introductions, yes. For grooms, most dogs behave better without their human audience — separation drama is real. The café over the road does an excellent flat white, and we text photos mid-groom.

How often should my dog be groomed?

Coat-dependent: every 4–6 weeks for poodle crosses and full-coated breeds, 8–12 for short coats and hand-stripped breeds. We'll give you an honest schedule and show you the brush-out routine between visits.

What if you find something during the groom?

Lumps, ear trouble, bad teeth, sore spots — we note them in your handover and tell you plainly if we think a vet should look. Groomers see more of your dog's skin than anyone; we treat that seriously.

Do you groom cats?

No — cats deserve a specialist, and we know a good one in Chorlton. We'll pass on the number without a flicker of judgement about your life choices.
