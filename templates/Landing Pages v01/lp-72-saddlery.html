STITCH 01 / THE WORKSHOPTWO NEEDLES, ONE THREAD

Two needles, one thread, no shortcuts.

The saddle stitch is the reason good leather lasts generations. Two needles pass through each hole from opposite sides, locking the waxed thread so that even if the stitching is cut, it won't run. A machine lock-stitch is faster and cheaper and unravels from a single broken loop. We stitch everything by hand, in a clamp called a pony, because the horse — and the customer — deserves the join that doesn't fail.

Bridle & Awl sits where it should: in Newmarket, among the yards. Half our work is equestrian — saddle fitting and reflocking, bridlework repair, the emergency restitch before a competition — and half is bespoke leather goods: belts, bags, dog leads and cases built to be handed down rather than replaced.

We work full-grain vegetable-tanned hide that ages into something better, cut and edged and burnished by hand. A well-made leather thing should look better in twenty years than the day you bought it. We build for that day, not for the sale.

STITCH 03 / THE DIFFERENCEWHY IT LASTS

Why hand-stitched wins.

01 / 03

Saddle-stitched, never machined.

Two needles, one waxed thread, a lock at every hole. Cut it anywhere and it holds. A machine stitch runs from a single broken loop — we don't use one on anything that matters.

02 / 03

Full-grain, vegetable-tanned.

The hide that ages into something better, not the coated split that cracks. We cut, edge and burnish by hand so the piece looks best years from now, not on day one.

03 / 03

Fitted to horse and rider.

A saddle is a fit, not a purchase. We check it on the actual horse and the actual rider, because the wrong fit hurts a back that can't tell you it's hurting.

Asked at the bench.

Why is hand saddle-stitching better than machine stitching?

A saddle stitch uses two needles locking the thread at every single hole, so if the stitching is cut or damaged anywhere it stays put — it physically can't run. A machine lock-stitch is one continuous thread that unravels from a single broken loop. It's slower to do by hand, which is why cheap goods don't, and it's exactly why ours outlast them.

My saddle might not fit my horse. Can you check?

Yes — saddle fit is core to what we do, and a poor fit is behind a huge amount of back soreness and behaviour that gets blamed on the horse. We check the fit on the actual horse and rider, reflock or adjust where we can, and tell you honestly if a saddle simply won't work for that back.

Can you repair leather I didn't buy from you?

Gladly — restitching, new billets, replaced buckles, split seams and worn edges on tack and goods alike. Bring us the inherited satchel or the tired saddlebag; most good leather is worth saving, and we'll give you an honest opinion (and a quote) before we start.

What leather do you use?

Full-grain, vegetable-tanned hide for the most part — the kind that develops a patina and lasts decades rather than the coated splits that crack and peel. It costs more and it's worth it; we'll talk you through the options and match the hide to how the piece will actually be used.

How long does a bespoke piece take?

A belt, a week or two; a bag or bespoke tack, several weeks — hand-stitching can't be rushed without becoming machine work. Equestrian emergency repairs before a competition we'll always try to turn around fast; tell us the date and we'll be straight with you about what's possible.

Do you sell ready-made goods too?

We keep a small shelf of belts, leads, and small goods ready to take away, but the heart of it is made-to-order and repair. If you want something specific, a quick conversation gets you exactly the piece rather than the nearest thing on a shelf.
