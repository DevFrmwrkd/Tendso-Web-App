PASS 01 / THE STUDIOBEAUTIFUL MISTAKES

The machine that makes mistakes beautifully.

A risograph is a strange and wonderful machine — a duplicator from the 1980s office world, repurposed by artists because it prints like nothing else. Each colour is a separate soy-ink drum, laid down one pass at a time, and the slight misregistration between passes is not a fault to fix but the entire charm. It's the warmth of screenprint at the speed of a photocopier.

We run two Risos in a bright studio off Stokes Croft, with a rack of drums from fluoro pink and orange to metallic gold and a deep federal blue. We print for illustrators, bands, poets, cafés and anyone who wants two hundred posters that look hand-made because they nearly are.

It's a limited palette and a limited process, and that's the point — the constraints are where the good work happens. We'll help you separate your artwork into spot colours, choose the paper, and embrace the overprints where the colours mix into a third. Perfection is not on the menu. Character is.

PASS 03 / THE PROCESSWHY RISO GLOWS

Why it looks like that.

01 / 03

Spot colour, not process.

No CMYK dots pretending to be photographs. Each colour is its own soy-ink drum, flat and vivid, laid one pass at a time. It's why Riso glows and inkjet doesn't.

02 / 03

The misregister is the point.

Slight overlaps and offsets between passes are the character of the process, not a defect. We design with them, not against them. Machine-perfect is a different shop.

03 / 03

Soy inks, kinder paper.

Soy-based inks and uncoated, recycled-friendly stock — the process is about as low-impact as printing gets, and it looks better for it. Bright and clean-conscienced.

PASS 04 / THE JOBART → SEPARATE → RUN → FINISH
  1. Step 01

    Send the artwork

    A finished design or a rough idea. Tell us the run size, the vibe, and any colours you're set on. We'll talk you through what Riso does brilliantly and what it doesn't.

  2. Step 02

    Separate & proof

    We split the art into spot-colour layers and print a proof so you see the real inks on the real paper — screens lie about Riso colour, always.

  3. Step 03

    Run the drums

    One colour, one pass, then the next drum on top. You're welcome to watch, and most people are quietly mesmerised by the second colour landing.

  4. Step 04

    Finish & collect

    Trimmed, folded, stapled or bound, counted and boxed. Editions get signed and numbered. Then you get to smell it, which everyone does.

What actually is risograph printing?

Think screenprint's colour and a photocopier's speed. Each colour is a separate soy-ink drum that prints one flat, vivid pass; you build an image by layering passes. It can't do photographic full-colour, but it makes spot colours glow in a way no inkjet or laser can, and the slight imperfections are exactly why people love it.

Can you print my full-colour design?

We can print a version of it — Riso works in spot colours, not photographic CMYK, so we'll separate your art into a handful of layers and often the result is more striking than the original. Send it over and we'll tell you honestly how it'll translate. Some images are born for Riso; a few really aren't, and we'll say so.

Why isn't it perfectly aligned?

Because it isn't meant to be. Slight misregistration between colour passes is the signature of the process — that soft offset and the overprints where two inks mix are the character you're paying for. If you need pin-sharp registration and flawless solids, digital or litho is your friend, and we'll point you to a good one.

What's the minimum run?

Riso earns its keep from around fifty copies upward — below that, digital is cheaper. There's no real maximum for us within reason; a few hundred is our happy place, and big poster runs are no trouble. Tell us the number and we'll tell you if Riso is the right call.

Can I learn to print myself?

Yes — open studio days run monthly, in small groups, and take you from artwork separation through drums and registration to a finished, printed thing you made yourself. No experience needed, and it's genuinely one of the most satisfying days out in Bristol. Fair warning: people get hooked.

What paper and inks do you use?

Soy-based inks and mostly uncoated, recycled-friendly stock — Riso is one of the greener ways to print, and the uncoated paper is what lets the inks look so good. We keep a range of weights and shades in the studio and will help you match paper to project when you visit.
