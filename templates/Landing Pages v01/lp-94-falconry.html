FIELD 01 / THE CENTREA PARTNERSHIP, NOT A CAGE

You don't tame a hawk. You earn a partnership with one, day by day.

Hawthorn & Hood is a falconry centre set in the woods and heather of the Northumberland hills, and it exists to share one of the oldest and most humbling relationships in the world — the one between a falconer and a bird of prey. We opened in 2006 with a handful of Harris's hawks and a conviction that these birds are best understood not through a cage at a distance but with one on your glove and the wind in the trees. Thousands of glove-first moments later, we still feel it every time.

Everything here is built around the welfare of the birds, and we won't ever apologise for putting them first, because a falconry bird is not a pet or a prop — it's a wild predator in a working partnership, and that partnership only exists on the bird's terms. Our hawks, falcons and owls are flown by weight and by trust, handled by experienced falconers, and never made to perform when they don't want to. We keep our groups small, our sessions unhurried, and our birds' wellbeing absolutely non-negotiable. Guests always feel the difference.

We offer hawk walks where a Harris's hawk follows you through the woods and flies to your glove, owl encounters that enchant children and adults alike, full falconry experience days for the truly bitten, and flying displays that show these birds doing what they were born to do. Whether you come for an hour, a day, or to stand in a field and watch a falcon stoop from the sky, you'll leave with a new respect for a creature that chooses, each time, to come back to the glove.

FIELD 07 / BEFORE YOU VISITSAFETY, WELFARE & THE DAY

Before you visit.

Anything else — ring 01434 555 118. We love talking about the birds, and we'll always be honest about welfare.

Is it safe, and will the bird hurt me?

Falconry with us is very safe when you follow the falconer's guidance, and injuries to guests are genuinely rare — but let's be honest about what these birds are, because that honesty is part of the respect. Hawks, falcons and owls are wild predators with sharp talons and beaks, and we never pretend otherwise; that's precisely why every session is supervised closely by an experienced falconer, why you're taught exactly how to hold the glove and stand, and why we read each bird's mood before it flies. Worn correctly, the falconry glove protects your hand where the bird lands, and the birds are focused on the food reward, not on you. Children are very welcome and we adjust everything for them. The golden rule is simple: listen to your falconer, do as they say, and you'll be completely fine — and you'll have one of the most thrilling wildlife experiences of your life.

Do the birds actually want to do this, or is it cruel?

This is the most important question anyone can ask, and we're glad when people do, because the welfare of our birds is the reason this centre exists in the form it does. Falconry, done properly, is not cruel — it's a working partnership rooted in the bird's own instincts, and the birds fly to the glove because they choose to, motivated by food, in a relationship built on trust and managed by careful daily weight-monitoring. A falconry bird that doesn't want to fly simply won't, and we never force one; if a bird is off-colour, moody, moulting or just not keen on a given day, it stays on its perch and we fly another or adjust the session. Our birds are not performing tricks; they're doing what they were born to do — hunt and fly — with a human partner. We'd rather cancel or change an experience than push a bird, and we do exactly that whenever needed. If you watch closely, you'll see birds in fine feather and good weight, calm and willing. That's not an accident; it's the whole point.

What should I wear, and does the weather stop it?

Dress for being outdoors in the Northumberland countryside whatever the forecast — layers, a warm top even in summer (it's breezy in the hills), and flat, sturdy, closed shoes or walking boots, as hawk walks cross uneven woodland ground. Avoid bright, flappy clothing and dangling accessories, which can unsettle the birds, and leave umbrellas behind (we'll advise on wet days). We provide the gloves and all the equipment. As for weather: falconry happens in most conditions and a bit of British drizzle rarely stops us, but we won't fly birds in genuinely unsafe conditions — high winds in particular are dangerous for the birds and we'll postpone rather than risk one. If we ever have to cancel for weather or for a bird's welfare, we'll rebook you at no charge. Come prepared for the outdoors and a little mud, and you'll be comfortable and free to focus on the birds.

Are experiences suitable for children, and is there an age limit?

Yes — many of our experiences are wonderful for children, and watching a child meet an owl or have a hawk fly to their glove is one of the joys of what we do. Owl encounters and shorter hawk walks are especially well-suited to younger visitors, and our falconers are brilliant with children: patient, engaging and safety-focused. We do set sensible minimum ages for the more involved experiences (handling larger birds needs a certain size and steadiness), and younger children are always closely supervised and helped by a falconer, sometimes sharing a glove with an adult. Full experience days suit older children, teenagers and adults. When you book, just tell us the ages in your group and we'll recommend exactly the right experience and explain what each child will be able to do. Family groups are some of our favourite bookings — there's something for everyone, and the grown-ups are usually every bit as spellbound as the kids.

Can I book a private experience, a gift, or a photography session?

Yes to all three. Private experiences give your group exclusive time with the birds and a falconer, ideal for families, couples, special occasions or anyone who'd prefer not to share the session — a lovely way to mark a birthday or anniversary. Gift vouchers are available for every experience, from a short owl encounter to a full falconry day, are open-dated and valid across the season, and make an unforgettable present for the nature-lover who's hard to buy for (we can send a gift pack). Photography sessions, in small groups or privately, are designed for keen photographers who want time and guidance to capture birds of prey in beautiful natural light — the falconer positions the birds and manages the flying so you can concentrate on the shot, and you'll come away with images most people never get the chance to take. Tell us what you're after and we'll build the right session.

Do you take in injured or unwanted birds of prey?

We're first and foremost a falconry and experience centre rather than a dedicated raptor rescue or hospital, so we can't always take in birds, and a genuinely injured wild bird of prey is best reported to the RSPCA or a licensed wildlife rehabilitator who can give it proper veterinary care and, ideally, return it to the wild. That said, we care deeply about the welfare of these birds beyond our own aviaries, and we're always happy to offer advice, point you to the right specialist rescue, and help where we sensibly can. We occasionally rehome captive-bred falconry birds whose owners can no longer keep them, when we have the space, the right setup and confidence we can give the bird a good life — this is assessed very carefully and never taken on lightly. If you've found an injured wild raptor or have a captive bird that needs a new home, do call us; even when we can't take the bird ourselves, we'll do our best to make sure it reaches someone who can help.
