DOCKET 01 / THE KITCHENONE OVEN, ONE COUNT

One oven. One count. Sessanta.

The oven came from Naples in a crate, was assembled brick by brick by a man who spoke only Italian and mild despair, and reaches 485°C at the dome. At that heat a pizza is done in sixty seconds — hence the name — and everything about the kitchen is arranged around that one number.

The dough is the argument we've had a thousand times and finally won: type-00 flour, a long cold ferment of forty-eight hours, salt, water, a whisper of yeast, nothing else. It's why the crust puffs into that blistered cornicione instead of sitting there like a cracker.

The menu is short because a Neapolitan menu should be. Marinara, margherita, a handful of specials the pizzaiolo will defend at length, and a nightly special that exists until the mozzarella runs out. No pineapple. We've made peace with losing that customer.

DOCKET 03 / NON-NEGOTIABLESALL THREE, FOR GOOD REASON

The non-negotiables.

1

The heat is non-negotiable.

485°C is what makes Neapolitan Neapolitan — the fast bake that puffs the crust and keeps the middle soft. A cooler oven makes a nice pizza. It doesn't make this one.

2

48 hours before you order.

The dough you eat tonight was mixed the day before yesterday. That slow cold ferment is why it's digestible, blistered, and worth the wait. Speed lives in the oven, not the fridge.

3

Short menu, defended.

Every pizza on the board has earned its place and can be argued for by name. We'd rather do eight things perfectly than forty things adequately, and the pineapple thing is final.

DOCKET 07 / ASKED AT THE PASSUSUALLY ABOUT THE FLOP

Asked at the pass.

Anything else — ring 0113 466 2280. Service is loud; leave a message and we'll ring back between bakes.

Why is the middle of the pizza wet / floppy?

Because it's Neapolitan, and that's correct. The soft, slightly wet centre is the style — you knife-and-fork the middle and fold the crust. A crisp-all-over base is a different, Roman thing, and a lovely one, but it isn't what this oven makes.

Do you take bookings?

Yes for tables, recommended at weekends. The bar and the six-seat chef's counter are walk-in only — arrive early or accept a lovely negroni's wait. Big groups: ring us, don't just turn up ten-strong on a Saturday.

Can you do gluten-free?

We do a proper gluten-free base, made to order — not brilliant like the real dough (we won't pretend), but genuinely good and taken seriously, with separate prep. Coeliacs, flag it when you book so we brief the kitchen.

Vegan and vegetarian options?

Loads vegetarian, and several genuinely good vegan pizzas with a house-made plant mozzarella that surprises people. The marinara is vegan by birth and arguably the purest thing on the menu.

Do you deliver?

No, and we mean it kindly. A Neapolitan pizza steams itself soggy inside fifteen minutes in a box on a moped. Collection is boxed with a vent and a stern look; eating in is always better. That's not gatekeeping, it's physics.

Is there really no pineapple?

There is really no pineapple. There is no secret pineapple. Asking increases the odds of a good-natured lecture on the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana. Everything else, we'll happily tweak.
