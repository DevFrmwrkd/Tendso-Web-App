REF. 01 / THE WORKSHOPABOVE THE ROWS · UP THE OLD STAIRS

One bench above the Rows.

The workshop sits above Chester's medieval Rows, up a staircase that predates the pendulum. On the bench: a 1962 timing machine that still tells the truth, a modern one that agrees with it, and whatever movement is currently in 292 pieces between them.

Training was five years at the British Horological Institute and a decade of other people's heirlooms since. The specialism, if forced to name one: watches that matter more than they cost. The grandfather's Omega. The wedding Seiko. The Smiths that went up Everest's little brother.

Every service is documented photographically — the movement before, during and after — because you're trusting a stranger with a time machine, and the least the stranger can do is show his work.

REF. 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERUSUALLY HOLDING AN HEIRLOOM

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 01244 318 552. Mid-assembly the phone waits; a hairspring outranks everything.

How much does a service cost?

Quoted per movement after assessment, never before — a three-hander and a chronograph are different animals. The assessment is free, the quote is written, and it doesn't move once you've agreed it.

How long will it take?

A straightforward service, four to six weeks; restorations longer, especially if parts need hunting. The bottleneck is doing it once, correctly, with a fortnight of timing tests at the end. We give real dates, not hopeful ones.

Is my watch worth servicing?

The bench answer: if it matters to you, yes. The honest answer for resale: sometimes no, and we'll say so. What we won't do is talk you out of fixing your grandmother's watch because a spreadsheet frowned.

Do you work on Rolex / Omega / vintage?

Yes, yes, and enthusiastically — with correct parts where obtainable and full disclosure where not. What we decline: fakes, franken-watches presented as original, and anything a previous "repairer" glued.

Will you replace the dial? It looks old.

We'd rather not, and we'll show you why: originality is most of a vintage watch's value and all of its face. Conservation over replacement, always — unless you overrule us in writing, which has happened twice in fourteen years.

Is the watch insured while you have it?

Fully, at declared value, from handover to handover — the certificate is on the wall and the values are reviewed annually. Sleep fine; the safe is better than ours at home.
