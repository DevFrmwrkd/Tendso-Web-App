KIT 01 / THE SHOPDEEP, NOT WIDE

We sell the kit. What we really deal in is the patience to finish it.

The Long Game is a model and hobby shop in the heart of Shrewsbury, run by people who build, paint and game themselves and can't help talking about it. We opened in 1998 when the town's last model shop closed, because a place like this — where you can hold the box, ask a real question, and get an answer from someone who's built the thing — is worth keeping alive. A quarter of a century later, the same customers' children are at the counter now.

We stock deep rather than wide: plastic and wooden model kits (aircraft, armour, ships, cars), model railways in the main scales with track, rolling stock and scenery, the paints, glues, tools and airbrush supplies that make or break a build, and a serious range of war-games miniatures, boards and paints. We only stock what we rate, we'll tell you honestly which kit is a joy and which is a fiddly nightmare for your level, and we'd rather sell you the right first kit than the most expensive one.

But what really makes us a shop rather than a website is everything around the kit: a paint bar where you can test colours before you buy, a members' build table where people work on projects and swap tips, beginners' advice given patiently and without a hint of the gatekeeping that puts newcomers off, and a noticeboard thick with clubs, swaps and layouts. Modelling is the long game — hours of quiet, careful pleasure — and we're the shop that keeps you in it for the long haul.

I've never built a model — what's a good first kit?

Welcome — first-timers are our favourite customers, and there's a real art to choosing a first kit that hooks you rather than defeats you, which is exactly the advice we love to give. For most adult beginners we suggest a good-quality kit at a larger scale (bigger parts are far more forgiving than tiny ones), of a subject you actually love — an aircraft, car, tank or ship that means something to you — because motivation carries you through the fiddly bits. Modern "snap" kits that need no glue are excellent for children and nervous starters, and many look superb finished. We'll steer you away from kits that are notorious for poor fit or a million tiny parts, make sure you leave with the right glue, a basic paint set and a decent knife, and show you where to begin. The goal is a finished model you're proud of, because nothing fuels a new hobby like a first success. Come and tell us what you fancy building and we'll set you up properly.

Do I need loads of expensive tools and paints to start?

No — and anyone who tells a beginner they need a huge, costly kit of gear before starting is doing the hobby a disservice. To build and paint a first plastic kit you genuinely need very little: the right glue (or nothing, for a snap kit), a sharp hobby knife, a cheap cutting mat, a small file or sanding stick, and a modest set of paints and a couple of brushes in the colours the kit calls for. That's it to begin. As you get keener you can add tools — better brushes, an airbrush, fancier putties and weathering products — but that should follow your interest, not precede it, and we'll always tell you honestly what's worth buying and what you can happily do without for now. We'd much rather you spent a little, enjoyed it, and came back for more than blew a fortune on kit that intimidates you. Start simple, build skill, and let the tool collection grow naturally with the hobby.

Can you help with model railways — starting one or extending one?

Absolutely — model railways are one of our deepest specialisms and among our greatest pleasures to help with, whether you're laying your very first oval of track or extending a layout you've tended for decades. For beginners (and for parents buying for children), we'll explain the main scales and what they mean for space and budget, recommend a train set or starter package that works properly out of the box, and set you on a path you can build on rather than a dead end. For established modellers we carry locos, rolling stock, track, controllers (including digital command control), buildings, scenery materials and the detailing bits that bring a layout to life, and we're always happy to talk through track plans, wiring headaches or that scenic technique you've not tried. The members' build table and our noticeboard of local clubs mean you can also tap into a whole community of railway modellers. Bring us your space, your scale and your dream, and we'll help you build it in achievable stages.

Do you stock war-games miniatures and paints, and can you advise a beginner?

Yes — we carry a serious range of war-games miniatures, boards, rulebooks, terrain and the full hobby paint ranges for the major systems, and crucially, the people behind the counter actually play, so the advice is real. Getting into miniature war-gaming can feel bewildering and potentially expensive, so we're honest with newcomers about what a given system really costs to start and to maintain, which starter sets genuinely represent good value, and how to build a force gradually rather than in one eye-watering go. We'll help you choose paints that suit your models and your patience, show you basic techniques (or point you to the build table and local gaming groups where you'll learn fastest), and steer you away from the classic beginner's mistake of buying far more than you'll paint. Whether you're painting a single display miniature or building an army to game with, we'll set you off sensibly and keep you supplied — and we love seeing works-in-progress brought back to the counter.

Is there really a paint bar and a build table I can use?

There is, and they're the beating heart of what makes us a shop worth travelling to rather than a website. The paint bar lets you test colours and finishes before you buy — invaluable, because a paint can look quite different on the model than on the label, and matching or planning a scheme is so much easier when you can actually try it. The members' build table is a space where you can sit and work on a project, whether you want the company, a second opinion, a borrowed technique, or simply somewhere to build that isn't the kitchen table; it's where a lot of friendships (and a lot of finished models) have started over the years. Membership is modest and comes with a few perks, but everyone is welcome to come in, browse, ask questions and soak up the atmosphere regardless. Pop in, see a build in progress, and you'll understand immediately why people keep coming back — it's as much a club as a shop.

Do you buy collections, or help with an inherited one?

Often, yes, and we're always glad to help people understand what they've got. We're interested in quality kits, model railway items and war-games collections, whether you're downsizing, clearing space, or have inherited a collection and have no idea of its value — and there's frequently more value in a well-kept collection than families expect, particularly with older railway items and rare, sealed kits. We'll give you an honest assessment and can buy outright, sell on your behalf, or simply advise you on the best route, including when a specialist auction would serve you better than we can. Unbuilt vintage kits in good boxes, discontinued railway stock and complete war-games armies are all of interest. If you've been left a loved one's collection and it feels overwhelming, bring us photographs or a boxful; we'll treat it and you with care, tell you honestly what it is, and make sure it ends up with people who'll value it as its owner did.
