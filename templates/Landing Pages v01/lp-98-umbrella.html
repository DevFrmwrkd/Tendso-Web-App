RIB 01 / THE SHOPKEPT, NOT BINNED

The world is drowning in broken umbrellas. We've spent thirty years bailing it out.

Gamp & Stick is an umbrella maker and repair shop in London, and we exist because of a small, infuriating truth: almost every umbrella sold today is designed to be thrown away. It turns inside out in the first proper gust, a rib snaps, and into the bin it goes — millions of them a year. We started in 1991 to make and mend the other kind: umbrellas built on a proper frame, with real materials, that you carry for decades and hand on.

The umbrellas we make are hand-framed the traditional English way — a solid stick, a strong steel or hickory frame, a tightly-tailored canopy, a handle turned or bound to last — and assembled and finished by hand in our workshop. They are heavier and dearer than the folding thing in your bag, and that is exactly the point: a Gamp & Stick umbrella is a lifetime object, weatherproof, wind-worthy and, frankly, a pleasure to be seen under. Buy once, carry for years.

But repair is half our soul. Bring us almost any umbrella of quality — a snapped rib, a canopy come away, a jammed runner, a handle worked loose, a beloved brolly your grandfather carried — and we'll very likely save it, often for a fraction of replacing it, and always in preference to the bin. We re-cover, re-rib, re-tip and re-handle; we make bespoke umbrellas for weddings and gifts; and we'll happily talk you out of buying a new one if yours can be mended. Kept, not binned.

Why buy an expensive umbrella when cheap ones exist?

Because the cheap ones aren't cheap at all once you count how many you get through, and because a real umbrella is a genuinely better object to own and carry. A supermarket folding umbrella is engineered to a price: light, flimsy ribs, a thin canopy and joints that give way in the first proper wind, which is why most people replace them constantly and why millions end up in landfill every year. A hand-framed umbrella is built the opposite way — a solid stick, a strong steel or hickory frame that flexes with the wind instead of snapping, and a tightly-tailored canopy — so it lasts for decades, survives the gales that destroy cheap ones, and can be repaired rather than binned when something eventually wears. Yes, it costs more upfront and it's heavier to carry; but over the years it works out cheaper, it keeps you genuinely drier, and it's simply a pleasure to use. Buy one good umbrella, look after it, and you may never need to buy another.

Can you really repair my umbrella, and is it worth it?

Very probably yes, and very often yes — repairing umbrellas is half of what we do and we save the great majority of those brought to us. The common failures are all fixable: a snapped or bent rib can be replaced or straightened; a canopy that has torn, perished or come away from the frame can be repaired or the whole umbrella re-covered onto its sound frame; a runner that jams or a spring that has gone can be freed or replaced; lost tips can be re-fitted; and a handle that has worked loose or cracked can be re-fixed or replaced. Whether it's worth it depends on the umbrella: for a quality umbrella, and especially for one with sentimental value, a repair is almost always worthwhile and far cheaper than an equivalent new one; for a cheap disposable brolly, we'll tell you honestly if it isn't worth mending. Bring it in and we'll assess it for free and advise you straight — we would genuinely rather mend your umbrella than sell you a new one you don't need.

What makes a handmade English umbrella different?

It's the frame and the making, and once you've carried one you feel the difference immediately. A traditional English umbrella is built around a single solid stick running the full length, with a frame of properly-gauged steel (or, on our finest, hickory) whose ribs and stretchers are engineered to open taut and to flex in the wind rather than invert and snap. The canopy is cut and tailored to fit that frame tightly, so it sits drum-tight when open and furls slim and elegant when closed. The handle — crook or knob, in hardwood, horn, cane or bound leather — is fitted to last and to feel right in the hand. All of it is assembled and finished by hand, rib by rib, in our workshop. The result is heavier and dearer than a folding umbrella, but it's weatherproof, wind-worthy, repairable, and frankly handsome — an object you're glad to be seen with and glad to hand on. It is to a supermarket brolly what a good pair of Goodyear-welted shoes is to a pair of flip-flops.

Do you make bespoke umbrellas for weddings and gifts?

Yes, and they're some of our happiest commissions. For weddings we make beautiful bespoke umbrellas — elegant clear ("birdcage") canopies that keep the rain off without hiding the dress and the smiles, or coloured canopies to match your scheme — in whatever quantity you need for a very British outdoor wedding, and unlike the flimsy ones hired elsewhere, ours are properly made and your guests will genuinely keep and use them afterwards. For gifts, a handmade umbrella (optionally personalised, and beautifully boxed) is a present that's practical, lasting and quietly luxurious — ideal for a retirement, a big birthday, a christening, or anyone who could do with being kept dry in style. We can work to your colours, your timescale and your budget, and we'll advise on what works best for the occasion. Talk to us well ahead for weddings and larger orders, and we'll make sure everyone's covered — literally.

Do you only make umbrellas, or walking sticks too?

Both — the two trades have always gone together, and we make, sell and repair walking sticks and canes in the same traditional, hand-finished way as our umbrellas. That includes everyday walking sticks fitted properly to your height and stride, handsome canes with turned, bound or horn handles, and the repair and re-handling of treasured and antique sticks whose handle or shaft has been damaged. Getting a walking stick right matters more than people realise — the wrong height or handle can do more harm than good — so we take the time to fit it correctly and advise honestly, exactly as we would with an umbrella. Whether you need a dependable stick for daily support, a fine cane for occasions, or the careful repair of one that's been in the family, bring it in or come and talk to us. It's all part of the same craft: well-made things, carried in the hand, built and mended to last.

Where are you, and can I post an umbrella in for repair?

Our shop and workshop are in London and we'd always love you to visit — it's the best way to choose a new umbrella, and it lets us assess a repair on the spot and advise you face to face. But we know not everyone can get to us, so yes, you're very welcome to post an umbrella in for repair or re-covering: get in touch first (a quick call or email, ideally with a photograph or two of the umbrella and the problem) so we can give you a rough idea of whether it's worth doing and what it might cost, and we'll tell you how to pack and send it safely. We'll assess it properly on arrival, confirm the price before doing any work, carry out the repair, and post it back to you ready for the next downpour. Whether in person or by post, the promise is the same: an honest assessment, a proper hand repair, and a strong preference for mending your umbrella over selling you a new one.
