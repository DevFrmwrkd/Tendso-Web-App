CUP 01 / THE TAHOTHE SOUND OF MORNING

Three ingredients, one call, and a whole country's childhood.

Taho is one of the simplest and most beloved things in Filipino food: warm, silken soybean curd, sweet dark syrup called arnibal, and chewy sago pearls, layered into a cup and eaten with a spoon or drunk straight down. It costs very little, it's sold on foot rather than in shops, and for generations of Filipinos the first sound of the morning wasn't an alarm — it was the magtataho calling "Tahoooo!" down the street.

Mang Ading has been that voice around these barangays since 2005. The soy curd is made fresh and kept warm in the traditional aluminium buckets, slung on a shoulder pole and carried on foot through the same morning route: past the school gate, the village, the jeepney stop, the houses where the kids already have their cups ready. The arnibal is cooked dark and caramel-deep, the sago boiled soft and chewy. Nothing fancy. Everything right.

What's new is that we now take bookings. Weddings, kids' parties, offices, school fairs and company events book the taho cart — Mang Ading, buckets, pole and all — for a nostalgic, unbelievably popular merienda station. We also do flavoured taho for those who want it, and take-home tubs of curd, arnibal and sago. But the heart of it is unchanged: warm taho, a fair price, and that call down the street in the morning.

What exactly is taho made of?

Three things, and that simplicity is the whole beauty of it. First, the base: warm, very soft silken soybean curd — essentially fresh, unpressed tofu, so soft it's spooned rather than sliced, with a delicate, faintly beany, comforting flavour. Second, arnibal: a dark syrup made from brown sugar (and sometimes a little vanilla), cooked down until it's rich and caramel-like, poured generously over the curd. Third, sago: small chewy pearls, similar in bite to tapioca, boiled soft and added for texture. They're layered together in a cup and eaten with a spoon or drunk straight down as the curd loosens. No dairy, no eggs, nothing complicated. It's naturally plant-based, high in soy protein, warm, sweet and comforting, and it costs very little — exactly why it's been a beloved everyday Filipino breakfast for generations.

Why is taho sold from buckets on a pole rather than from a shop?

Because taho has always been a mobile, street-sold food, and the delivery method is genuinely part of the tradition. The magtataho — the taho vendor — carries two large aluminium buckets suspended from a wooden pole balanced across the shoulders: one holds the warm soy curd, the other holds the arnibal and sago, along with the cups and ladle. He walks a regular route through neighbourhoods, calling out 'Tahoooo!' as he goes, and people come out to the gate or the roadside with coins in hand. The whole system exists because taho is best warm and freshly ladled, it's cheap enough that it needs no overheads, and it goes to where people actually are in the early morning. For most Filipinos, that call down the street is inseparable from the food itself; it's a sound that means morning.

Is taho healthy? Is it vegan?

Taho is naturally plant-based — the curd is made from soybeans, the arnibal from brown sugar, and the sago from starch pearls — so it contains no dairy, meat or eggs, and it's generally suitable for vegans and for people avoiding lactose. Nutritionally, the soy curd is genuinely good: a decent source of plant protein and low in fat, which is part of why taho has long been considered a wholesome, filling breakfast for workers, students and children on modest budgets. The honest caveat is the arnibal — it's sugar syrup, and a generous pour makes taho a fairly sweet drink, so it's a treat as much as a health food. The good news is it's easily adjusted: ask for less arnibal (or less sago) and you'll get a lighter cup while keeping all the warm, silky soy goodness. Mang Ading is happy to pour to taste.

What time do the morning rounds happen, and can you come to my street?

Taho is fundamentally a morning food, and the rounds run early — that's when people want it, and when the curd is at its freshest and warmest. Mang Ading walks a fairly consistent route through the barangays each morning, hitting the same landmarks around the same times, which is how regulars know roughly when to listen for the call. Because the route can vary a little with the day and the weather, the easiest thing is to message us to ask where he'll be and roughly when. And yes — you can absolutely request a stop on your street or at your village: if there's enough interest from a few households or a village gate, we're glad to add or adjust the route. For anything guaranteed at a specific time, book the station instead.

Can I book taho for a party, wedding or office event?

Yes, and it's become one of the most popular things we do — booking the taho station brings the whole nostalgic experience to your event: Mang Ading, the buckets, the pole, and warm cups served fresh to your guests for an agreed period. It works brilliantly at kids' birthdays (children go wild for it and it's mercifully mess-free compared to most party food), weddings and reunions (older guests are frequently moved by it), school fairs, store openings and corporate merienda breaks. It's also refreshingly affordable compared to conventional dessert catering while being far more memorable. To book, message or call with your date, location, expected headcount and time window, and we'll confirm availability and sort quantities, cups and whether you'd like flavoured arnibal alongside the classic. Weekends, holidays and December book up fastest.

Do you have flavoured taho or take-home options?

Yes to both, though we'll always be a bit partial to the classic. Alongside traditional brown-sugar arnibal, we offer flavoured syrups — ube, strawberry, chocolate and pandan are the usual suspects — over the same warm silken curd, which kids in particular love and which makes a fun extra option at events. And for taking home, we can pack the components separately — curd, arnibal and sago in their own containers — so you can assemble fresh cups at your own table whenever you like, warm the curd gently, and pour to your own taste. That's ideal for households that want taho on their own schedule, for gifting, or for anyone whose street isn't on the morning route. Order take-home tubs ahead and collect, or add them onto an event booking.
