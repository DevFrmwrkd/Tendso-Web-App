FOLIO 01 / THE SHOPA SHOP YOU'RE MEANT TO WRITE IN

A shop you're meant to write in.

Most pen shops keep the good pens behind glass and hand you a scrap of till roll to test on. We think that's backwards. At Nib & Ledger the writing table is the centre of the shop — sit down, dip in, and find out how a nib actually behaves on decent paper before a penny changes hands.

A nib is a personal thing. The same pen writes wet for one hand and dry for another, broad for a heavy presser and fine for a light one. You cannot know from a photograph or a forum. You can know in about ninety seconds at the table, which is why the table exists.

Behind the counter is the repair bench, where the grandfather's Parker with the bent nib and the dried-solid Sheaffer come back to life. We stock inks by the two hundred, paper that respects them, and the patience to help you fall down the whole rabbit hole slowly.

FOLIO 03 / HOUSE RULESWRITTEN IN INK

Three rules, written in ink.

RULE I

Write before you buy.

Every pen, on real paper, with your chosen ink, at the table, for as long as you need. A nib is too personal to buy blind — so we don't let you.

RULE II

Paper tested with a wet nib.

We don't stock a notebook until we've hit it with a broad, wet nib and a saturated ink. If it feathers or bleeds through, it never reaches the shelf.

RULE III

The bench brings pens back.

Inherited, bent, dried-solid, written-off — most pens aren't dead, just neglected. We assess for free and fix what's fixable, honestly about what isn't.

FOLIO 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERPEN IN HAND

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 01225 447 209. If we're mid-repair we'll call back once the sac is seated.

I've never used a fountain pen. Where do I start?

At the table, with us next to you. Fountain pens are easier than their reputation — lighter pressure, better angle, and suddenly writing stops being a chore. We'll start you on a forgiving nib and an inexpensive pen that punches far above its price. No one starts with the Montblanc.

Can I really test the expensive pens?

Yes, any of them, at the table, with your ink of choice. A pen you're going to keep for twenty years deserves ninety seconds of your handwriting first. We'd rather you bought the right £40 pen than the wrong £400 one.

Do you repair pens I didn't buy from you?

Absolutely — most of the bench work is exactly that. Inherited pens, junk-shop finds, the one that's been in a drawer since 1998. Assessment is free; we'll tell you honestly whether it's worth the work before we start.

What's a nib grind and do I need one?

Reshaping the nib tip to change how it writes — an italic for line variation, a stub for flair, an architect for crisp verticals, or just smoothing a scratchy one. You don't need one to enjoy a pen, but if you write a lot, it's transformative. Try a ground nib at the table and see.

Which ink won't wreck my notebook?

That's our favourite question. We test every paper we stock against wet, saturated inks, and we'll match an ink to your notebook so it doesn't feather, bleed or ghost. Bring the notebook you love; we'll find the ink that loves it back.

Do you post inks and refills?

Yes — inks, samples, refills and paper go out UK-wide, and the ink-sample subscription is quietly popular. Pens we'd rather you came and wrote with first, but we'll advise over the phone for anyone who genuinely can't make it to Bath.
