HEAT 01 / THE FORGETWO HEARTHS, ONE HAMMER

A forge, not a fabrication shop.

There's a difference and it shows. A fabrication shop cuts stock steel, bends it cold and welds it square. A forge heats the metal until it moves like clay and shapes it under the hammer — which is why hand-forged work has a life to it that folded box-section never will.

Cinder & Anvil is a two-hearth forge on the edge of Frome. Everything leaves here having been hot: the leaf on a gate, the twist in a baluster, the taper on a poker. We draw it, we forge it, we finish it, and the person who quoted your job is the person swinging the hammer.

We take on more small commissions than most forges bother with — a single fire set, a pair of gate hinges, a house name in forged letters. The big architectural jobs pay the rent; the small ones keep the craft honest.

Asked at the forge.

Isn't hand-forged just expensive fabrication?

No — it's a different process. Fabrication bends cold stock and welds it. Forging heats steel until it moves and shapes it under the hammer, which is slower, skilled, and gives the work a quality you can see from across a room. You're paying for the fire and the hand, not a markup.

How long does a commission take?

A fire set, a couple of weeks. A pair of gates, six to ten weeks depending on the drawing and the queue. Big architectural jobs are quoted with a real programme, not an optimistic one. We'd rather give you a date we'll hit.

Do you work from my drawing or your own?

Either. Bring a sketch on a napkin, a Pinterest board, or a full architect's package — we'll turn it into a scale drawing you approve before anything's heated. If it won't work in steel, we'll tell you before you've paid for it.

Can forged ironwork survive outdoors?

For generations, finished properly. We hot-zinc galvanise or use traditional oil-and-wax depending on the piece and the exposure. Coastal jobs get the belt-and-braces treatment. Ask about the finish; it's half the lifespan.

Do you do small jobs?

Gladly — more than most forges. A single hook, a house name, a repair to one hinge. The small jobs keep the hand in and often turn into the big ones later. No job is too small to be worth doing properly.

Can I watch my piece being made?

On larger commissions, yes — arrange a visit and we'll time it for the interesting part. The forge is a working, hot, loud place, so it's boots and sensible clothes, and children only by arrangement. It's genuinely worth seeing.
