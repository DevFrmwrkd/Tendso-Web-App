BOT. 01 / THE DISTILLERYAGNES, 450 LITRES, COPPER

One gin. That's the whole portfolio.

Most distilleries our size launch a rhubarb edition by year two and a coffee liqueur by year three. We decided early that Ninebark would make one gin and spend every year making it slightly better. Batch 1 was good. Batch 214 is better in eleven documented ways.

The recipe is public — all nine botanicals, their origins, their weights per hundred litres. It's framed on the tasting-room wall. A recipe isn't the hard part of gin; the hard part is cuts, patience, and tasting every batch against the last one at nine in the morning.

The still is called Agnes, after the founder's grandmother, who is on record as finding the whole enterprise "a bit much". She came to the opening anyway. Her portrait supervises the bottling bench.

BOT. 03 / CONVICTIONSFRAMED, LIKE THE RECIPE

What we'd defend in court.

№ 1

The recipe is public.

All nine botanicals, origins and weights, framed on the wall and printed on the label. Confidence is a better moat than secrecy.

№ 2

Every batch tasted against the last.

Nine a.m., same glasses, same order, notes in the batch ledger. Two hundred batches of small corrections is why batch 214 beats batch 1.

№ 3

One product, zero gimmicks.

No pink edition, no glitter, no gin "inspired by" a season. The absence of a flavoured range is our quality guarantee.

BOT. 07 / ASKED AT THE BARUSUALLY AFTER THE SECOND CUT

Asked at the bar.

Anything else — ring 020 8533 7741 or message us. If the still is mid-run, we answer between cuts.

Is the tour suitable for someone who doesn't drink?

Yes — the process is the show, and it's genuinely interesting sober. We make a proper 0% botanical distillate for the tasting, not a sad lemonade, so non-drinkers taste along at every step.

Can I really see the full recipe?

It's framed on the tasting-room wall: nine botanicals, origins, weights per hundred litres. Photograph it if you like. The recipe was never the hard part — ask us what is, it's the best question on the tour.

Why don't you make a flavoured gin?

Because we'd rather make one thing excellently than five things adequately. Every January we taste the market's new releases, feel briefly tempted, and go back to work on the cuts.

How do I visit?

Book online or ring. Tours run Friday and Saturday; the tasting room opens Friday evenings without a booking. We're two minutes from Hackney Wick Overground, in the yard with the crane mural.

Do you ship bottles?

UK-wide, yes, from the current batch. Londoners can collect and get the batch-ledger tour thrown in. We'd always rather hand you the bottle.

Can we hire the space?

Evenings, up to thirty people, with or without a tasting. It's a working distillery — the room smells of juniper and the still has right of way. Most people consider both features.
