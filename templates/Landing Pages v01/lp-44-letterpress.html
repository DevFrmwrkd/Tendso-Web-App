GALLEY 01 / THE STUDIOCATHERINE HILL, FROME

Three tonnes of press. Zero pixels.

Pica & Quoin runs on two machines with more working history than most streets: an 1893 Wharfedale stop-cylinder that arrived through the window on a crane, and a Heidelberg Windmill that sounds — regulars agree — like a very confident heartbeat.

Everything is set by hand from the studio's forty cases of type, or plated from your artwork, then printed with real impression. You can feel the letters. That's not a flaw in the process; it is the entire process.

We take on a handful of commissions a month and keep the rest of the diary for workshops, because the fastest way to explain letterpress is to put your hands on the press and pull.

Why iron wins.

1

Impression you can feel.

Digital printing kisses the paper. We hit it — calibrated, deliberate, deep. The difference is the whole reason this studio exists.

2

Set by hand, every job.

Forty cases of wood and metal type, or your artwork made into plates. Either way a human sets, inks and pulls every single sheet.

3

Presses with pedigrees.

The Wharfedale is 130 years old and prints truer than machines a century younger. We are its custodians, not its owners.

GALLEY 07 / ASKED AT THE STONEANSWERED MID-PULL

Asked at the stone.

Anything else — ring 01373 452 918. If the Windmill's running we genuinely cannot hear you; try again in ten.

Why does letterpress cost more than digital?

Because a human sets, inks and pulls every sheet on a three-tonne machine, and because the result gets kept, framed and rubbed between fingers. You're not buying printing; you're buying the only card in the stack anyone remembers.

How long does a commission take?

Three to five weeks depending on the diary and the number of colours — each colour is a separate full run through the press. Weddings: book eight to ten weeks out and everything stays calm.

Can you print my design?

Almost certainly — we make photopolymer plates from clean artwork. Fine lines and solids love the press; photographic gradients don't. Send the file and we'll tell you straight, usually the same day.

What's the minimum run?

Fifty for cards and stationery, twenty for posters. Below that the makeready outweighs the run — better to come to a workshop and print it yourself, honestly.

What happens at the workshop?

Six people, one day, all hands. You'll set type from the cases, lock up a forme, mix ink, and pull your own small edition. Wear things that forgive ink. It gets under fingernails and into personalities.

Do you sell prints?

The shelf by the door carries current editions and the community posters. First Thursday evenings, whatever's on the Windmill that night is yours to pull and take.
