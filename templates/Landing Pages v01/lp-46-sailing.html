MARK 01 / THE WATERINSIDE THE BAR · OUTSIDE WHEN EARNED

Learn where the water is kind. Mostly.

The Salcombe estuary is the perfect classroom: flat and forgiving inside the bar, honest and gusty at the mouth, and never more than a short reach from the slipway and the kettle. We've taught eight-year-olds, eighty-year-olds, and one memorably determined stag party.

Leeboard runs on a fleet of thirty boats — Picos and Fevas for the small and fearless, Wayfarers for families, RS Quests for courses, and two keelboats for the weekends when the estuary feels too small.

All instruction is RYA-syllabus, all instructors are qualified and first-aid current, and the rescue boat is crewed every single session by someone whose only job is watching you. Capsizing isn't failure here; it's Tuesday.

Adult Level 1 & 2

Two days from zero to sailing a triangle unassisted. The most common review: "why did I wait twenty years?"

M3

Better sailing clinics

Spinnakers, trapeze, racing tune-ups, heavy-weather confidence. For sailors with the basics and an appetite.

Keelboat weekends

Two days on our Sonatas out past the bar — tides, pilotage, and lunch on a mooring. The gateway drug to yacht ownership.

M5

Family own-boat tuition

Your boat, your crew, our instructor aboard. We untangle the boat and, frequently, the family.

M6

Schools & groups

Term-time programmes for local schools and youth groups, subsidised by the summer courses. The fleet earns its keep both ways.

MARK 07 / ASKED ON THE SLIPWAYUSUALLY IN A NEW WETSUIT

Asked on the slipway.

Anything else — ring 01548 843 771. If we're on the water, the answerphone knows the tide times.

Do I need to be able to swim?

Comfortable in the water in a buoyancy aid is the requirement — fifty metres is plenty. Everyone wears buoyancy at all times, ours, provided, properly fitted, no exceptions including instructors.

What happens if I capsize?

You get wet, you right the boat, you carry on — it's taught as a drill in week one precisely so it becomes boring. The rescue boat is beside you before you've finished being surprised.

What should I bring?

Old trainers, a towel, swimwear, sun cream and a water bottle. We supply wetsuits, spray tops, buoyancy aids and the boats. In April, bring enthusiasm; in August, bring more sun cream.

Am I too old to learn?

Our oldest Level 1 graduate was 78 and bought a Wayfarer the following spring. Sailing is tactics and feel, not strength — the boat does the work once you stop fighting it.

Is it safe for kids?

RYA ratios, DBS-checked instructors, crewed rescue cover, and a strict wind limit for each stage. The honest risk profile: cold, tired, hooked for life.

What if there's no wind? Or too much?

No wind: theory, tows, and rope work that will genuinely change your life. Too much: the decision is ours, made early, and courses extend or credit — we don't send beginners out to be brave.
