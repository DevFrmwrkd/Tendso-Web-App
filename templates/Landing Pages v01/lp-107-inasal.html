SKEWER 01 / THE GRILLBACOLOD, THE HOME OF INASAL

Inasal without chicken oil is just grilled chicken. And we don't serve just grilled chicken.

Inasal sa Uling grills chicken inasal the Bacolod way — and Bacolod is where chicken inasal comes from, so we don't take that lightly. Inasal means "grilled," but real Bacolod inasal is a whole ritual: chicken cut into parts, marinated for hours in kalamansi juice, lemongrass, garlic, ginger, vinegar and annatto oil that stains it a deep orange, then grilled over uling — coconut-shell charcoal — while being basted again and again with garlic-infused chicken oil until the skin chars and glistens.

We opened in 2012 with a single long grill and a marinade recipe from a Negrense grandmother who would accept no substitutes: real kalamansi, real tanglad (lemongrass) pushed into the cavity, annatto steeped in oil, nothing from a bottle labelled "inasal mix." Every order is grilled fresh over the coals — you can smell it from the street — and served with the holy trinity of Bacolod eating: unli rice, sinamak (spiced coconut vinegar) for dipping, and a pot of chicken oil to pour over your rice with abandon.

We grill the classic cuts — pecho (breast), paa (leg), and the beloved offal skewers of isaw, atay (liver) and pakpak (wings) — plus grilled pork and seafood, and pancit molo and cansi for the Negros table. It's counter-service, casual, and gloriously messy: eat with your hands, dip in the sinamak, ask for more rice, and understand at last why Bacolodnons are so smug about their chicken.

Why it tastes like home.

01 / 03

Grilled over uling, not gas.

Coconut-shell charcoal, a long grill, and constant hand-basting — the smoke and char are the flavour. Gas-grilled "inasal" is a pale imitation, and we won't do it.

02 / 03

Marinated from scratch.

Real kalamansi, lemongrass, garlic, annatto oil, marinated for hours — never a shortcut powder. The deep orange and the tang come from the marinade, the way Bacolod intended.

03 / 03

The chicken oil is the secret.

That golden, garlicky rendered chicken-and-annatto oil, basted on the grill and poured on your rice, is what separates real inasal from grilled chicken. We give it freely. Drown your rice.

    Order at the counter

    Pick your cuts — pecho, paa, a stack of isaw and atay — and say how many rice. It's casual counter service; grab a table and we'll fire it on the grill fresh for you.

What makes Bacolod chicken inasal different from regular grilled chicken?

Everything, honestly — inasal just means "grilled" in Hiligaynon, but Bacolod chicken inasal is a specific, beloved tradition and not simply chicken over fire. The difference starts with the marinade: hours in kalamansi juice, lemongrass, garlic, ginger, cane vinegar and annatto (achuete) oil, which gives real inasal its tangy, herbaceous flavour and deep orange colour. Then it's grilled over uling — coconut-shell charcoal — for that particular smoke, and basted repeatedly with garlic-infused chicken oil, which crisps and glazes the skin. Finally it's served with unlimited rice, sinamak vinegar for dipping, and a pot of chicken oil for the rice. Take away any of those elements and you've got grilled chicken, not inasal. Bacolod is the acknowledged home of the dish, which is why we're fussy about doing it properly.

What is the chicken oil, and why does everyone rave about it?

Chicken oil — often just called "the oil" by regulars — is the golden, garlicky, annatto-tinted rendered fat that is the soul of a Bacolod inasal meal. It's made from chicken fat and skin rendered slowly with garlic and annatto, and it does double duty: we baste the chicken with it on the grill, which is part of why the skin gets that glossy char, and we serve a pot of it at the table to pour over your rice. That last part is the one people get evangelical about — plain white rice, unlimited and hot, drenched in garlicky chicken oil, is genuinely one of the great simple pleasures of Negrense eating. We give it freely because being stingy with the chicken oil is a betrayal of the whole tradition. Pour it on with confidence.

What are isaw, atay and the offal skewers?

They're the grilled chicken innards and extra parts, skewered on sticks, and they are a genuine cult favourite — for many inasal lovers, the true test of a grill house. Isaw is chicken intestine, cleaned thoroughly, marinated and grilled until slightly charred and chewy-crisp; atay is liver, richer and softer; there's also balunbalunan (gizzard), and pakpak (wings). Marinated in the same inasal flavours and grilled over the coconut coals, dipped in sinamak, they're smoky, savoury, cheap and completely addictive. If you didn't grow up eating offal they can sound intimidating, but grilled inasal-style they're one of the most approachable ways to try it — start with the atay if you're nervous. And if it's really not for you, the chicken cuts are the main event.

Is it really unlimited rice?

Yes — unli (unlimited) rice is part of the deal, and it's not a gimmick; it's essential to how inasal is eaten. A plate of smoky, tangy, chicken-oil-glazed inasal practically demands rice, and lots of it, so we keep it coming as long as you're eating, at no extra charge. The pattern is simple and joyful: order your inasal, get your rice, pour on chicken oil, and when the rice runs out before the chicken does, just ask for more. Between the unlimited rice, the affordable grilled cuts and the free-flowing chicken oil and sinamak, an inasal meal is one of the most satisfying-per-peso meals you can eat, which is exactly why it's such a beloved everyday food across Negros. Come hungry; the rice will meet you there.

Can I order inasal for a party or take it away?

Definitely — take-out and party orders are a big part of what we do. For take-away, we grill your order fresh and pack it with sinamak and chicken oil on the side so nothing goes soggy and you can dress it at home; it travels well, though inasal is always best straight off the coals. For parties and offices we do bilao platters of assorted cuts and skewers, a generous, crowd-pleasing spread. Because everything is grilled to order over charcoal, we ask for a bit of notice on larger orders so we can grill fresh to your pick-up or delivery time. Tell us the headcount, the cuts you want and when you need it, and we'll sort quantities, the chicken oil and sinamak, and a fair price.

Where in Bacolod are you, and are you tourist-friendly?

We're in Bacolod City in Negros Occidental — the home of chicken inasal — and yes, very much visitor-friendly, though we're a local grill house rather than a polished chain, which is rather the point. If you're in Bacolod for the MassKara Festival or just passing through, eating inasal at a proper local uling grill like ours is one of the essential food experiences of the trip, and we're used to welcoming first-timers and happily explaining the cuts, the skewers, the chicken oil and the whole ritual. It's casual counter service, cash-friendly, gloriously messy and meant to be eaten with your hands. Our exact location and current hours are on our Google Business Profile with a map pin; follow the smell of charcoal and grilling chicken.
