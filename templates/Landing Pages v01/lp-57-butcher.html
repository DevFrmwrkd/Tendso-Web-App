CUT 01 / THE SHOPNORTH STREET, BEDMINSTER

Nose to tail, or don't bother.

Marrow & Bone buys animals whole, from farms within an afternoon's drive that raise native breeds slowly on grass. We know the farm, the breed, and often the field. That relationship is why the meat is better and why we pay a price a farmer can actually live on.

Buying whole means we're committed to the whole animal. Anyone can sell you a fillet; the craft is making the bavette, the ox cheek, the featherblade and the bones sing just as loudly. Ask us what to do with a cut you've never cooked — that conversation is the actual product.

The dry-ageing happens in the window, on the bone, for weeks — because you should be able to see it, and because a cabinet full of ageing beef is the most honest advertising a butcher has.

CUT 03 / THE CREEDCHALKED ABOVE THE BLOCK

The block's creed.

1

Named farm, native breed.

Every animal comes from a farm we can name, a breed we can name, raised slowly on grass. If we can't tell you where it lived, it doesn't hang in our window.

2

Whole animal, no waste.

Buying the whole beast means using the whole beast — the cheap cuts and the trim get the same care as the fillet. That's better value for you and better ethics for the farm.

3

The advice is the product.

The bavette, the cheek, the shin — the best-value meat in the shop is the stuff you've never cooked. Tell us your kitchen and we'll change how you eat for the price of asking.

CUT 07 / ASKED AT THE COUNTERUSUALLY MID-QUEUE

Asked at the counter.

Anything else — ring 0117 442 6690. Saturdays are chaos; leave a message and we'll ring back between beasts.

Isn't a proper butcher expensive?

The prime cuts, a little. But buying whole means the shop is full of brilliant cheap cuts most people never try — bavette, cheek, shin, chuck — that cost less than supermarket mince and eat like a treat. Ask us; the value lives in the cuts you don't know yet.

Where does the meat come from?

Named farms within an afternoon's drive, native breeds, raised slowly on grass. We can tell you the farm and usually the breed for anything in the shop — it's chalked on the board and we're proud of it, so ask.

What's dry-ageing and why does it cost more?

Hanging beef on the bone in a cold, controlled window for weeks — it loses water and gains deep, nutty flavour and tenderness, and loses sellable weight, which is the cost. Thirty days is lovely; sixty is for the committed. All visible in the window.

I can't cook. What should I buy?

Tell us that, honestly — it's the most useful thing you can say. We'll point you at something forgiving with a foolproof note: a slow-cooked shin that can't really go wrong, or a steak with exact timings. Confidence is built one good cut at a time.

Do you do boxes and delivery?

A monthly whole-animal box, mixed across the beast with cooking notes, for collection or Bristol delivery. One-off orders we'll set aside for collection; ring ahead for anything special or large so we cut it fresh.

Can you handle a big order — party, hog roast, Christmas?

Yes, and the sooner the better — whole animals take planning and the good birds sell out. Christmas turkeys, hog roasts, a whole rib for the table: tell us the headcount and the date and we'll build it around a real animal.
