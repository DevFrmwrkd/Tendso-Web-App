WEAVE 01 / THE WORKSHOPMANDAUE, FURNITURE CITY

Rattan is a grass, not a tree. Worked right, it's stronger than furniture has any right to be.

Tadhana Rattan is a furniture workshop in Mandaue, Cebu — the city that turned the Philippines into one of the world's great rattan-furniture makers, exporting the handwoven pieces you've seen in homes and resorts everywhere. Rattan is a climbing palm, a solid, flexible cane that grows fast in our forests; steamed, it bends into curves no timber could manage, and once cooled it holds that shape with astonishing strength. We've been bending and weaving it here since 1988.

The craft is genuinely a craft. We heat the solid rattan poles with steam and bend them by hand around forms to build the frame — the graceful loops of a peacock chair, the arc of a headboard, the sweep of a sofa arm. Then the weavers take over, wrapping joints and binding panels with split rattan skin, cane and abaca in the tight, even patterns that are the whole beauty of wickerwork. It's slow, skilled, human work. A machine cannot bend a pole to the eye or weave a curve to the hand.

We make furniture to order for homes, interior designers, resorts and hospitality; we restore and re-cane heirloom and vintage rattan that's sagged or split; and we export worldwide, because Philippine rattan is prized far beyond our shores. Rattan is also genuinely sustainable — a fast-growing, renewable vine, not a slow hardwood — which is part of why the world is coming back to it. Commission a piece and it'll be woven by hand to last for decades, then re-woven when it finally needs it.

What's the difference between rattan and wicker — and is rattan the same as bamboo?

These get mixed up constantly, so here's the clear version. Rattan is a material: a climbing palm (a solid, flexible vine-like cane) that grows in tropical forests, and it's the raw stuff furniture is made from. Wicker is not a material at all — it's a weaving technique, the process of weaving pliable material into furniture. So a piece is made of rattan (or cane, or abaca, or even synthetic fibre) and woven in the wicker style; 'wicker furniture' just means woven furniture. Bamboo is different again: a hollow grass with rigid, jointed stalks, whereas rattan is solid all the way through and far more flexible, which is exactly why rattan can be steam-bent into the flowing curves of a peacock chair while bamboo tends to be used in straighter constructions. In short: rattan is the solid bendable cane we build with, wicker is how we weave it, bamboo is a different hollow plant. We work primarily in real rattan, with cane and abaca for the weaving.

Is rattan furniture actually durable, or is it delicate?

Properly made rattan furniture is genuinely durable — often surprisingly so — which is why it's been prized for generations and why antique rattan is still in use. Solid rattan pole, steam-bent and well-jointed, makes a strong, resilient frame; it has a bit of natural give that helps it absorb stress rather than crack, and good hand-woven binding is tight and load-bearing, not decorative. The 'delicate' reputation usually comes from cheaply made pieces with thin poles, sloppy weaving, or damage from misuse and the elements. Treated well, a quality rattan chair comfortably lasts decades. The honest caveats: natural rattan is happiest indoors or in covered outdoor areas, as prolonged direct sun and rain will dry, fade and weaken it (for fully exposed outdoor use, synthetic all-weather wicker is the better tool); and like all fine furniture it benefits from care. But within its element, well-made rattan is a workhorse, and when it does wear, it can be repaired.

Can you make furniture to my own design or measurements?

Yes — made-to-order is central to what we do, and a real advantage of buying from a genuine workshop rather than off a showroom floor. Because every piece is built and woven by hand, we can work to your specific dimensions, proportions, weave pattern, frame style, finish colour and cushion fabric, whether you're matching an existing set, fitting an awkward space, realising a designer's drawing, or reimagining a classic in your own way. Come with as much or as little as you have: a precise technical drawing, a photo of something you love, or just a description of the room and the feeling. We work regularly with interior designers and architects on bespoke and contract pieces, so we're comfortable reading plans and hitting a specific brief and finish. For anything custom we'll usually confirm details carefully, and often sample the weave or finish, before we start.

Do you restore old or heirloom rattan furniture?

Yes, and it's some of the most satisfying work we do. One of the best things about well-made rattan is that it's designed to be repaired rather than thrown away — the frame typically long outlives the woven parts, so a piece that looks 'finished' is very often thoroughly rescuable. We re-cane sagged or broken seats, reweave split or unravelled binding and panels, re-glue and re-lash loosened joints, and refinish tired surfaces, bringing heirloom peacock chairs, planter's chairs, vintage sofas and family pieces back to full strength and beauty. Because we make rattan from scratch, we can match weave patterns, cane thickness and finishes closely to the original, so a restoration blends in rather than looking patched. If you've got a family piece that's sagging, splitting or wobbling, don't assume it's beyond help; bring it in or send clear photos of the whole piece and the damage, and we'll assess it honestly.

Is rattan an eco-friendly, sustainable choice?

Broadly, yes — rattan is one of the more genuinely sustainable materials in furniture, and part of its appeal today. Rattan is a fast-growing climbing palm that matures far more quickly than hardwood timber, regenerates after harvesting, and grows naturally within tropical forest ecosystems, which means well-managed rattan harvesting can provide income without clearing forest — it can actually give standing forest economic value that encourages its preservation. It's renewable, biodegradable, and lightweight (which lowers shipping emissions). As with anything, sustainability depends on responsible sourcing rather than the material alone, and we care about that. It's also worth being honest about synthetic 'all-weather' rattan, which is a plastic woven to look like rattan: more weatherproof for exposed outdoor use, but not natural or biodegradable. We work primarily in real, natural rattan, and if outdoor durability is your priority we'll talk you through the trade-offs.

Can you ship a piece to me abroad?

Yes — export is part of our heritage, not an afterthought. Cebu built its reputation as a global furniture hub partly on exporting handwoven rattan around the world, and we crate and ship pieces to homes, interior designers and buyers internationally as a matter of course. Rattan has a real advantage here: it's relatively lightweight for its size, and many pieces can be built or partly knocked down to pack efficiently, which keeps freight sensible. When you order from abroad, we'll advise honestly on what ships well, what can be flat-packed or nested, lead times, and how the piece should be crated and protected so it arrives in perfect condition. For designers and trade buyers placing larger or repeat orders, we're set up for contract-scale export with consistent quality across a run. Whether it's a single peacock chair to your apartment overseas or a container of matched sets for a project, tell us what you need and where it's going, and we'll walk you through it.
