01 / THE STUDIOTWO STREETS FROM THE SEA

Eight wheels. Three kilns. Zero precious attitude.

Cone Six is named for the kiln temperature most of our glazes mature at — about 1220°C, which is also roughly the temperature of a beginner's frustration in week two, right before it all clicks.

The studio runs three lives at once: courses for people who've never touched clay, a members' bench for the hooked, and kiln services for potters across Thanet who need reliable firings without owning a kiln.

Nobody here floats around saying "just feel the clay". You'll be shown where your elbow should be, why your cylinder is a bowl, and how to fix it — in that order.

07 / ASKED AT THE SINKWHILE WASHING UP

Before you get muddy.

Anything else — ring 01843 229 461. Hands are usually wet; leave a message and we'll ring back dry.

I've never touched clay. Where do I start?

The taster session. Two hours, everything provided, aprons included because you will need the apron. If it clicks — it usually clicks — course dates are released monthly.

What do I get at the end of a course?

Three finished, food-safe pots of your choice from everything you make, glazed and fired. The rest gets recycled back to the clay bin, which is character-building.

How does the firing service work?

Drop off Tuesday, collect from Friday. Bisque and cone-six glaze firings priced by shelf; odd sizes quoted on sight. Work must be bone dry — we check, because explosions are communal events in a kiln.

What's included in membership?

Bench space, wheels, the house glaze library, a storage shelf, and firing at member rates. Clay is bought from us at cost. Two tiers; the anytime tier includes a door key and the accompanying sense of ownership.

Can I buy the pottery?

The seconds shelf sells members' and teachers' work with minor flaws at friendly prices. Restocked most Saturdays. Firsts go to the makers' own shops and markets — we'll point you at them.

Is it suitable for kids?

Family hand-building sessions run in school holidays, ages 6+. The wheel needs a bit more leg, so wheel courses start at 14.

I have wrist/back issues. Can I still throw?

Often yes — throwing can be adapted (standing wheels, lighter clay weights). Book a one-to-one first and we'll find what works before you commit to a course.
