WRAP 01 / THE SHOPTHREE FAMILIES, ONE BATTER

Three families, one fryer, since 1962.

Batterhouse opened the summer of 1962 and has been run by three families since — each one trained by the last, each one refusing to change the batter. The recipe is in a safe. The safe is a biscuit tin. The biscuit tin is guarded by Doreen, who has worked the counter since 1989 and outranks everyone.

The haddock comes up the hill from the harbour market; the potatoes come down from Wolds farms we've dealt with for decades — Maris Pipers most of the year, and we'll tell you honestly when the new-crop months make the chips softer, because you'd taste it anyway.

Beef dripping is non-negotiable. It fries hotter, crisps better and tastes like the twentieth century's best decision. (There's a separate vegetable-oil fryer for the fish-free and the faithful of other persuasions — separate oil, separate tools, properly done.)

The fryer board.

01 · Haddock & chips

The reason the queue bends round the rail. Harbour haddock, dripping-fried, batter that shatters. Regular feeds a person; large feeds a person who's been swimming.

02 · The full counter

Cod for the traditionalists, scampi, fishcakes made in the back, sausages battered and not. Mushy peas in proper tubs, curry sauce with opinions.

03 · The sit-down room

Forty seats, bread and butter, pots of tea, the good vinegar on every table. The window seats watch the bay; the corner seats watch Doreen.

04 · The gluten-free fry

Tuesday and Thursday, dedicated pans and batter. Coeliac-safe done seriously, not as a shrug — book nothing, just come.

05 · Veggie & vegan fryer

Separate oil, separate tools: battered halloumi, "fish" of battered banana blossom that surprises people weekly, and the same chips as everyone.

06 · Crew & party orders

Trawler crews, rugby teams, office Fridays, one wedding (the bride queued in the dress). Ring ahead for anything over ten and we'll stage the frying.

Why the queue bends.

1

Fish that came up the hill.

The harbour is four hundred metres away. Our haddock's journey is shorter than your walk from the car park — and you can taste the difference that makes.

2

Beef dripping, held the line.

Everyone else switched to oil in the nineties. We didn't, because dripping fries crisper and tastes better, and sixty-three years of queues agree.

3

Chips from named farms.

Wolds Maris Pipers, same families for decades, rumbled and cut here every morning. When the crop changes, the board says so. Honesty is house policy.

WRAP 07 / ASKED IN THE QUEUEANSWERED OVER THE RANGE

Asked in the queue.

Anything else — ring 01723 355 662. Dinnertime rush, Doreen answers in under six words.

Haddock or cod?

Haddock. This is Yorkshire; cod is on the menu for visitors and we fry it beautifully, but haddock is the house religion — sweeter, flakier, and what the harbour lands. Doreen will serve you cod without judgement. Visible judgement.

What's in the batter?

Flour, water, a raising agent and a sequence Doreen guards in the biscuit tin. No beer — beer batter is for gastropubs. The crispness comes from the dripping's heat and sixty years of not fiddling.

Do you fry in beef dripping? Is there an alternative?

Yes, proudly, since 1962 — it's why the batter shatters. And yes: a fully separate vegetable-oil fryer with its own tools for vegetarian, vegan and preference orders. Separate means separate here.

When's the gluten-free fry?

Tuesdays and Thursdays, all day — dedicated pans, dedicated batter, coeliac-safe procedures we'll happily describe in detail. No booking; the board outside confirms it's a GF day.

Can I eat in?

Forty seats, pots of tea, bread and butter for the chip butty of your life. Sea-view window seats go first. The room is cash-or-card, the queue outside is faster, the gulls work both.

What about the gulls? Seriously.

Seriously: back to the wall, food close, never hold a chip aloft in triumph. The gulls of Scarborough are professionals and they have studied you. We sell a lid for a reason.
