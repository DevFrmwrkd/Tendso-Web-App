BUILD 01 / THE SHOPLAS PIÑAS, JEEPNEY TOWN

Every jeepney is one of a kind. That's not a slogan — it's how we work.

Sarao Kings Body Works is a jeepney fabricator in Las Piñas, the town synonymous with jeepney-making. The jeepney is the Philippines' most iconic vehicle — born from surplus US military jeeps after the war, stretched, roofed and reborn as public transport, and then, unmistakably, decorated: chrome, mirrors, painted scenes, streamers, horses on the hood, and the driver's family names down the side. It's less a bus than a rolling declaration, and no two are ever the same.

We've been hammering, welding and painting them since 1979, and we still do it the traditional way — forming stainless-steel body panels largely by hand, fitting the engine and running gear, then handing the shell to our chrome men, upholsterers and, most importantly, our painters. The hand-painted art is the soul of the jeepney: the lettering, the landscapes, the saints and slogans, the family names. A machine can't give you that. A craftsman who's done it for forty years can.

Today we build new jeepneys for operators, fully restore vintage ones for collectors and museums, fabricate custom bodies and art for brands, films, resorts and events, and take on the chrome, paint and upholstery jobs that keep a working jeepney proud. The traditional jeepney faces an uncertain future on the roads, which makes the craft matter more, not less. As long as someone wants one built right, the King of the Road rolls on — and we'll be the ones who built it.

What exactly is a jeepney, and why is it decorated like that?

The jeepney is the Philippines' most iconic mode of public transport and its most recognisable cultural object on wheels. It originated after the Second World War, when surplus American military jeeps were rebuilt by ingenious local hands — stretched to add rows of bench seating, fitted with a roof, and turned into cheap, shared public transport. Over decades it evolved its own unmistakable style: gleaming stainless-steel bodies, banks of chrome ornaments, rows of side mirrors, chrome horses mounted on the hood, and, above all, exuberant hand-painted decoration — landscapes, religious imagery, slogans, pop-culture references and the operator's family names down the sides. The decoration isn't random flash; it's a genuine folk-art tradition and an expression of the owner's identity, faith, family and pride. Each jeepney is effectively a rolling personal statement, which is why no two are alike.

Do you build brand-new jeepneys, or only repair and decorate?

Both — we're a full body works, so we do everything from a complete new build to individual chrome, paint and upholstery jobs. A ground-up new build means we form the stainless-steel body largely by hand, fit the engine and running gear, install the seating and interior, add the chrome and ornaments, and finish with the hand-painted art and lettering, delivering a complete, road-ready jeepney built to spec. But we also restore old and vintage jeepneys, and take standalone jobs — re-chroming, a new paint scheme, fresh lettering, re-upholstering the seats — for owners who want to keep their existing jeepney proud. And we build custom, sometimes non-running showpiece jeepneys for brands, films, resorts and events. Tell us what you've got and what you want, and we'll scope it.

How long does a jeepney build take, and roughly what does it cost?

It varies enormously with the scope, so we quote each project individually after understanding what you want. A jeepney is coachbuilt largely by hand, not stamped out on a production line, so a full new build or comprehensive restoration is a matter of weeks to months rather than days, depending on the complexity of the bodywork, the amount and intricacy of the chrome, and especially the ambition of the hand-painted art, which is skilled, time-consuming work. Cost similarly depends on size, mechanical specification, materials, how much chrome and ornamentation you want, and the extent of the custom artwork; a simple refresh is a modest job, a fully custom new build with elaborate art is a serious commission. Rather than quote a misleading figure blind, we'd much rather talk through your vision and budget and give you a clear, honest quote and timeline.

Is the artwork really hand-painted, and can I choose the design?

Yes, genuinely hand-painted — the artwork and lettering are brushed on by hand by painters who have been doing this specific craft for decades, and it's the part of the work we're proudest of. This is the true folk-art heart of the jeepney tradition: the landscapes, the religious imagery, the pinstriping, the decorative lettering and the all-important family names, all rendered by a human hand, which is exactly why they have the character, boldness and one-of-a-kind quality that no machine-cut vinyl wrap can replicate. And yes, you absolutely choose the design — it's your jeepney and, traditionally, an expression of your identity. Whether you want the classic repertoire (scenery, saints, family names), a specific theme, a brand's colours and logo, or something entirely your own, our painters will work with you, sketch it up, and bring it to life.

Can you build a jeepney for a brand, film, resort or event?

Absolutely, and it's some of our favourite work. A jeepney is an incredibly powerful visual — instantly, unmistakably Filipino, gloriously eye-catching, and rich with cultural meaning — which makes it a phenomenal centrepiece for a brand activation, a film or TV production, a resort or restaurant, a museum, or a special event. We can build a fully custom jeepney in your colours and branding with bespoke hand-painted art, restore and re-liver an existing one, or fabricate a static, non-running showpiece designed purely to look spectacular parked at your venue or launch. Clients consistently find the jeepney out-photographs and out-remembers almost anything else at an event. Bring us your idea, timeline and budget and we'll tell you honestly what's achievable and how.

With jeepney modernization, does traditional jeepney-building still have a future?

It's a real and complicated question, and we won't pretend otherwise. The traditional jeepney does face an uncertain future as public-transport modernization programmes push towards newer, standardised, often factory-built vehicles, and that genuinely threatens the old coachbuilding trade and the communities that grew up around it. We take that seriously. But we also believe the craft itself — the hand-formed steel, the chrome work, and above all the hand-painted folk art — has enduring value well beyond its role as a transport vehicle: as heritage, as art, as identity, and as something collectors, cultural institutions, brands and proud individuals will always want built and kept alive. So our work is increasingly about restoration, preservation, custom and commemorative builds. As long as people value the King of the Road as the icon it is, there's a future in building it right.
