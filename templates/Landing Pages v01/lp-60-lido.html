LENGTH 01 / THE LIDORESTORED, TILE BY TILE

Ninety years. One length at a time.

Sundial opened in 1934 as one of the great seaside lidos, closed in the lean years like most of them, and reopened after a community trust spent a decade and a great deal of stubbornness bringing it back. The fountain works again. The clock on the diving-board wall keeps time. The paint is the original teal, matched from a chip found under sixty years of repainting.

The pool is a full fifty metres, heated to twenty-six degrees so the season runs March to November, and open to the sky the whole time. Lane swimmers have it from six; families from noon; and on the longest evenings we open late and the water goes gold.

It's run as a trust, not a chain. Every ticket keeps the boiler on and the paint fresh, and members' names are painted, one per tile, along the plant-room wall. There is a waiting list for a tile. There is not, yet, a waiting list to swim.

LENGTH 02 / SESSIONSROPED BY PACE

Pick your lane.

Lane 1 · from 06:00

Lane swimming

Six lanes, roped by pace, from 6am. Fast, medium and "social" lanes honestly labelled and gently policed. The dawn slot is a cult; join it.

Lane 2 · from noon

Family afternoons

The shallow end, the fountain on, floats from the bin, and a lifeguard who means it. The best-value afternoon on the coast, weather no object.

Lane 3 · all season

Membership

Annual and monthly, swim as often as you like, and eventually your name on a tile along the plant-room wall. The only loyalty scheme we've ever loved.

Lane 4 · all ages

Lessons & squads

Learn-to-swim for all ages, adult improvers who never quite got the hang of it, and a masters squad that races and mostly wins.

Lane 5 · seasonal

Sunrise & late swims

Dawn lanes and long-evening late openings when the water turns gold. Announced on the board, worshipped by regulars.

Lane 6 · dry

The sun terrace & kiosk

Deckchairs, decent coffee, an ice-cream hatch, and the finest people-watching in Margate — swim optional, though you'll cave.

LENGTH 03 / WHY SUNDIALTHREE THINGS THE SEA CAN'T DO

Why a heated tank beats the sea.

01 / 03

Heated, so the season is long.

Twenty-six degrees, March to November, under open sky. Not a summer novelty — a pool you can swim before work in April and after work in October.

02 / 03

Fifty real metres.

A proper Olympic-length tank, not a leisure puddle with a flume. Lanes for swimmers who count lengths and a shallow end for everyone still learning to.

03 / 03

Owned by the town.

A community trust, not a leisure operator. Every ticket keeps a listed 1934 lido alive; profits become paint, boiler parts and another summer open.

LENGTH 04 / YOUR FIRST SWIMBOARD → BOOK → IN → TERRACE
  1. Step 01

    Check the board

    Water temp, session times and lane setup posted daily — on the gate, online, and on the seafront webcam for the truly keen.

  2. Step 02

    Book or walk up

    Lanes can be booked; family afternoons are walk-up. Members skip the queue and swim on impulse, which is the whole point of membership.

  3. Step 03

    In you get

    Heated changing village, hot showers that stay hot, and towels for hire if you forgot. The cold shock is optional and character-building.

  4. Step 04

    Stay on the terrace

    Deckchair, coffee, ice cream, and the sea over the wall. Half our swimmers stay longer on the terrace than in the water. No notes.

LENGTH 07 / ASKED AT THE GATEUSUALLY WHILE TOWELLING OFF

Asked at the gate.

Anything else — ring 01843 220 550. The webcam on our site shows the water right now, if you're deciding.

Is the pool really heated?

To twenty-six degrees, all season, which runs March to November. It's warm enough that the dawn swimmers get steam off the surface on a cold morning — one of the great sights of a Margate spring. The changing village and showers are heated too.

Do I need to book?

Lane sessions can be booked and fill fast at peak times, so booking is wise. Family afternoons are walk-up — just turn up. Members can book earlier and skip the queue, which most decide is worth it by about their third visit.

What if it rains?

We open anyway, and honestly it's a highlight. You're already wet, the water's warm, and the terrace has cover for the coffee. We close only for thunder and lightning — lifeguard's call, and it's final.

How cold is too cold — when do you close?

The pool is heated, so weather rarely closes us; the season boundaries are about daylight and running costs, not swimmer bravery. High winds or electrical storms clear the water. Otherwise, dress warm on the walk in and you'll be glad you came.

Can children swim?

Of course — family afternoons from noon, a proper shallow end, floats to borrow, and lifeguards throughout. Under-eights need an adult in the water within arm's reach; that's the one rule we never bend.

How does membership work?

Monthly or annual, unlimited swims, priority booking, and a genuine hand in keeping a 1934 lido alive. Long-standing members eventually get their name painted on a tile by the plant room — there's a waiting list, and it's the good kind of problem.
